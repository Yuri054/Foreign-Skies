# Foreign Skies: what the game is for

Read this before changing any rule. When a choice is between this file and realism, this file wins.

## The game

A sandbox on a map of Europe from 1900, in the spirit of Age of Civilization / Age of History: you take any nation, even
the smallest, and write your own story with it. The favourite way to play is peaceful: develop the country, and fight
only when the story calls for it. There is no end date; a game may run for centuries.

## Rules every system answers to

1. **Your effort compounds.** Building and investing pay back within a few years, and what they earn pays for the next
   round. Given time, a one-province country can become the largest economy in the world. Growth may be "broken" by
   the standards of history; that is the point.
2. **You can see it.** Every lever shows its effect within a year, in a number, a ranking, the map or the news. A
   menu whose effect you cannot see is a bug.
3. **The world grows too, but less well than you.** The AI builds without a plan, so a focused player outgrows it.
   How well the AI builds is what difficulty changes.
4. **Peace is a full way to play.** The world reacts to you but rarely forces your hand. Nations behave consistently:
   a friend stays a friend unless something happens, and the game says what happened.
5. **Readable like CK3, not like Victoria.** Every number says why it is what it is, and every choice says what it
   will do before you make it.
6. **Choices cost something.** An army costs what growth would have bought; a building costs money. You cannot have
   everything at once, but you can have anything eventually.

## Not goals

- Historical accuracy where it gets in the way of the rules above. History is the starting point and the backdrop: the
  1914 crisis may come, but the world the player has built decides who matters by then.
- Simulation depth for its own sake (goods, markets, pops), and micromanagement.

## How a change is judged

By playing, not only by the AI-only measurement tools. A change names the rule it serves and the playtest it should
pass ("Serbia, at peace, building steadily: a top-10 economy by 1950"), and is done when it passes it.
