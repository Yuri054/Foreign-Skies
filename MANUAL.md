# Foreign Skies: a player's guide

Foreign Skies is a sandbox of Europe from 1900 to the great war and after. Nothing ends it and nothing scores you: you
run a nation and the AI runs the rest. This guide says what the screens are for and how the pieces fit. The keys are in
the README and in the pause menu (Esc).

## Starting

On the main menu choose **New game**, a scenario (January 1900, at peace; or July 1914, with the crisis under way) and a
**difficulty**: *Historical* (the nations play as they were trained), *Challenging* (bolder, take worse odds, stand by their
allies, quarrel sooner) or *Sandbox* (slower to quarrel, quicker to make peace). The difficulty never gives anyone more than
the rules give; it retunes how the AI behaves. **Tutorial** is a guided first game in 1900. Music and sound live under
**Settings**.

Then pick a nation. Pause with Space; the game runs in real time at five speeds.

## The map

Left click selects a province, an army or a fleet; a box drag selects several; right click gives an order to what is selected,
or opens the country panel on another nation. The map has modes (keys 1 to 6): political, diplomacy (who likes whom, from any
nation's point of view), economy, population, military (control, front line, strength, **supply**) and terrain. **V** turns on
the 3D map. Hover a province for its card; with armies selected the mouse also shows what an order there would do (see
*Armies*).

## The Nation screen (N)

One window, a tab for each part of running the nation:

- **Overview**: the **cabinet briefing** (the three things most worth your attention, each with the reason and a button to
  the tab that deals with it), your figures, Europe's tension (what it is made of, the worst rivalries, a chart of two years)
  and your wars with what each is being fought for.
- **Economy (E)**: money, taxes, spending, industry, food, raw materials. Money pays the army, interest and what the state buys.
- **Build (B)**: pick a building, then click a green province. **Buildings cost industry (IC), not money**: the nation has a
  number of IC a day, projects are served in order and wait when the day's IC runs out, so the treasury never moves when you
  build. The queue shows why a project is "stalled" (no IC reaches it).
- **Military (M)**, **Navy (Y)**: armies, army groups, fronts, fleets, yards.
- **Diplomacy (F)**: opinions, treaties, wars, gifts, justification, demands, peace.
- **Focus (O)**: one national effort at a time, with lasting effects.
- **Rankings (R)**: every nation side by side, with charts.
- **History (H)**: the history of the run, year by year, the wars, the changes of the map and how nations stand; it can be
  exported as a document or a web page.

## Armies

Select armies and right-click a province. The mouse tip shows the days the road takes, the date of arrival, how much of the
way is out of supply and what you can see of an enemy there (a range from your intelligence, never the exact men, with the
odds on men alone: terrain and trenches are not counted). An army out of supply loses men; the **Supply** map variant shows
where your armies are fed (green), where supply thins (amber) and where it ends (red). Armies dig in the longer they stand
still; defenders in trenches and on rough ground are hard to dislodge, and an attacking division that enters an undefended
province takes it at once.

**Fronts** are lines of provinces armies hold. Hand armies to a front (the command panel, C, or the Military tab) and they
spread along it and hold it; an order of your own takes an army off its front. Armies can be grouped under a field marshal.
In a battle you are in, the battle screen has a **staff report**: how it goes, how long each side lasts, and what a quarter
more power, a weaker enemy or another level of trench would do.

## Diplomacy

Almost everything costs **diplomacy points**, earned monthly (more with prestige): treaties (alliance, guarantee,
non-aggression, passage), gifts, improving relations, justifying a war.

**War needs a reason.** A war with no casus belli costs prestige, goodwill and war support, and needs a mobilized country.
Better to have a goal:

- Some goals come with the scenario: a **claim** (France on Alsace-Lorraine), which gives a casus belli on its provinces; a
  defence of a guarantee or an ally.
- **Justify war** (Diplomacy tab) lets you choose what the war will be for: named provinces, or a concession (reparations, a
  withdrawal of the border armies, an end to the enemy's ties to someone). It takes diplomacy points and some weeks, the
  target and its friends hear of it and think less of you, and you then hold a casus belli with that goal. At peace the goal is
  half price and everything else dearer, so a war for Liège is easy to end with Liège and hard to end with Brussels.
- **Press a demand** (Diplomacy tab) opens a **crisis** instead of a war: you ask for a province or reparations and the
  nation has until a deadline to yield, refuse (you then hold a casus belli for it), mobilize (if you cannot win, you back
  down) or appeal to the powers.

**Crises** also arise by themselves between rivals when tension and grievances build: a demand with a deadline. If it concerns
you, you are asked at once. If it does not, you may **mediate** (a chance from your prestige and the goodwill you hold with
both; success eases tension and earns prestige) or **back** a side. A war with many nations fought at once is closed at a
**conference**: your allies at war with the same enemy sit with you, each asks what its own goal and score pay for, and the
enemy takes the whole package or refuses (it may offer less).

## Events and tension

Europe's **tension** (top bar) rises with the arms race, mobilization, rivalry and recent crises; the bolder the AI nations
grow, the nearer it gets to 100. Historical events stop time and put choices to you: each option shows its **largest effect**
and, on hover, everything it does. In the Overview you can make a public stand to calm or rattle Europe.

## Saving

F5 and Shift+F5 quick-save and quick-load; F6 opens the save window. The game saves itself on the first of each month into three
rotating slots. A save keeps the difficulty it was made at.

## Tips

- Read the cabinet briefing each month; it is the quickest way to see what is wrong.
- Build before the money runs out: construction is paid in industry, not money, but industry comes from your factories.
- Do not send armies beyond supply without a plan to take ground that will carry them.
- A claim is cheaper than a pretext, and a goal makes the peace you want cheap.
- Allies pull you into wars as they pull the enemy's allies in: look at who is bound to whom before you declare.
