# Foreign Skies: Design

A historical-pressure sandbox on a map of Europe. There is no score and no victory screen. You run one of 22 nations through the years in which the great powers drifted toward war, and what you do, and what the others do, decides whether the great war comes. What the game is for, and the rules every system answers to, are in `VISION.md`; this file describes the game as it is; `README.md` says how to run and test it.

## Pillars

- **The map is the interface.** A flat 2D map (or a 3D one with real relief, a moving sea, cities, and armies as small figures on stands in their nation's colour, fleets with their wakes), map modes that carry most of the information, and one Nation screen for everything about running the country.
- **History pushes, it does not script.** Crises and events arrive on a calendar, but every one is a choice and the outcome depends on the state of the world. Whether 1914 ends in a general war is not decided in advance.
- **A few systems that fit together.** Money, industry and manpower are the whole economy. Diplomacy, war and the AI read the same numbers.

## Scenarios

| | Start | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| January 1900 | 1 Jan 1900, peace | The long campaign: 1900 borders (Albania and Macedonia Ottoman, Southern Dobruja Bulgarian), smaller populations and armies, no submarines, the Franco-Russian and Triple Alliances, no Entente. Historical pressure steers the AI (`pressure_ai`), as in 1914. |
| July 1914 | 28 Jul 1914, Austria-Hungary at war with Serbia | The short start, with the crisis already under way and tension at about 70, so the AI is already bold. The tutorial is played in 1900, not here. |

A scenario is a folder in `data/scenarios/` with `scenario.json` (nations, deployment, alliances, guarantees, relations, claims, `owner_changes`, `start_tension`, `pressure_ai`), and `provinces.tsv` (people), `resources.tsv`, `navy.tsv`, `cities.tsv`. `tools/make_1900.py` builds the 1900 folder from the 1914 one and holds the numbers that differ.

## Systems

### Economy (`scripts/sim/economy.gd`, `data/economy.json`)
Three pillars, each with a number to read and a lever.
- **Money.** Income is tax on output (lever: the tax rate) plus tariffs at ports and the sale of surplus raw materials. It pays civil government, the army and navy, imports, debt interest and state investment. The treasury may go negative; debt costs interest. `Economy.calibrate` sets each nation's civil spending so a scenario starts with a small surplus (`start_surplus` of revenue) to build or arm with; after that civil spending grows with the people and the baseline drift (`Nation.civil_index`), not with what buildings, foci and investment add, so growth reaches the budget, and mobilization, blockade and arms production take their share of it as of the output. A soldier costs `soldier_upkeep` times the nation's output per head at the start against `soldier_upkeep_ref` (`Economy.wage_factor`, within `wage_min` and `wage_max`), rising after only with the baseline drift (`Nation.wage`, `Economy.soldier_wage`), so a poor nation's army is cheap and a rich one's dear, every nation starts with its army paid for, and growth does not make the army dearer. Above the default rate the tax strain rises evenly toward the maximum (`Economy.tax_strain`), costing growth (`tax_strain.growth_penalty`) and war support; under it the *tax relief* adds growth (`tax_relief.growth_bonus`) and a little support, so every rate does something. *State investment* (`SetEconomyPolicy` "investment", at most a tenth of the month's output) is a monthly sum spent like any cost that adds yearly growth of `invest.growth_per_share` times the share of output invested, easing a little past `soft_share`, with no ceiling: the engine of the snowball (VISION.md). Since civil spending and soldiers' pay do not grow with the economy, a growing nation's surplus grows faster than its output, and invested it compounds. The AI invests at most `ai.json` budget invest_max_share of its output, so a focused player outgrows it. `Economy.growth_rate` adds the baseline, the focus, the tax and investment.
- **Industry.** Industrial output sets building capacity (IC), spent a day at a time on one queue of buildings and ships. The small works everywhere add `construction.base_ic` a day for an economy of `base_ic_gdp_ref` or more and a share of it for a smaller one (never under `base_ic_min`), so IC follows size; and works are built to the size of the place: a province with less output than `cost_ref_gdp` pays that share of a building's cost (`Economy.size_factor`, never under `cost_min_factor`), and a factory, which multiplies industry, is measured on the province's industry against `cost_ref_industry` (`size_by`), so a factory pays back its IC in about 15 years in a Serbian town or an Italian one and faster only in the great industrial cities. A level is also paid for in money when it is begun (`gold_share` of its IC at `ic_value`; none for trenches and fortifications), so building competes with the army for the surplus; the Build tab shows, for the best place now, the cost and what the budget gains a year (`Economy.level_gain`, after the raw materials the new industry needs). Raw materials are one ratio (own deposits plus imports against need) that scales industry; shortfalls are bought abroad by themselves, so materials and food matter when a blockade cuts the imports (the Economy tab shows them under "Imports and the blockade"). Food is a separate famine check.
- **Manpower.** Recruits from population are drawn on to raise units and refilled each month. The *standing army* (`Nation.peacetime_army`) is what is kept under arms at mobilization 0: the player sets it (`SetEconomyPolicy` "standing_army") up to `standing_share` of the manpower pool (`Economy.standing_cap`); the AI's grows with its people and output while its budget is in surplus, to the same cap. The Economy tab says what a division costs a month (`Economy.division_upkeep`), how many the surplus keeps and the treasury raises, and the briefing names money lying idle in peace. Mobilization is the lever: more men under arms, lower factory output. In peace it may reach 40%; at war it is pushed up to a war floor. What it costs a month, the reservists' upkeep and the output lost less the civil spending that falls with it, is worked out before it moves (`Economy.mobilization_cost`) and shown on the slider. In peace units can be sent home (`DemobilizeUnits`: an army, a count or "to peacetime", guns last); their men go to the reserve, and past what mobilization keeps back to the people at the month's end, and upkeep falls at once. The AI sends home a share (`ai.json` forces.demob_share) of what it has above its peacetime army times `demob_margin` each month from `demob_after_days` after its last war, while not mobilized.
- **Munitions** (`scripts/sim/munitions.gd`, `economy.json` munitions). A nation puts its *arms share* of each day's IC into a stockpile before the hulls and buildings; the share also takes output (`output_penalty`: the factories making shells do not make the people's goods). The stock ages (`decay_per_year`) and costs money to keep (`storage_cost`). In battle an army's guns (artillery's power) burn `per_gun_power` a day; an army of a nation at war next to an enemy army burns `idle_share` of that. A nation that cannot meet the day's need fights with its guns weakened by `combat.shell_penalty` times the share missing (`Munitions.burn`, `power_factor`, in `Military._fight` and the staff report's forecast). Munitions bought abroad (`BuyMunitions`) cost `import_price`, cut by the blockade, so money and the sea count in the land war. A stockpile's *days of war* are at the expected use (last month's, or `engaged_share` of what every army would burn fighting at once). The AI arms by tension, crisis and war (`ai.json` munitions) and buys when its stock runs low. With a typical peacetime share the great powers run short within months of a general war: the shell crisis comes out of the rules.

Buildings: factory, railway, port, trench, fortification. National focus (`data/focus.json`) is one effort at a time that adds lasting or one-off effects: the seven great powers each have a tree of their own (16 to 19 foci in four or five branches, written by `tools/make_focus_trees.py`), every other nation a short common one. Every tree ends in repeatable foci (`repeatable`, `max`; `max` 0 for ever) whose effects are one-off or capped, so a nation whose tree is done still has a national effort in hand; the AI takes them only once nothing else can be started (`AIFocus.step`). Big cities begin with the factories and railways they already had (`buildings` in the scenario, from `tools/make_start_buildings.py`), which is why they smoke.

### Military (`military.gd`, `navy.gd`, `data/military.json`, `data/navy.json`)
Infantry, cavalry and artillery in armies under generals, grouped into army groups. Combat uses strength, terrain, entrenchment and morale, which makes defence strong. Cavalry is fast and weak in a fight, but it takes ground after a battle in `occupation.cavalry_mult` of the terrain's time and harries a beaten army as it falls back (`Military.pursuit`: `combat.pursuit` times the victors' share of cavalry, at most `pursuit_max`, on top of `routed_loss`). Armies need supply from owned or occupied land. Fronts are lines of provinces armies hold; attack lines send an army group along one. On the player's fronts each army deploys as detachments (`Army.parent_id`), one stack to a province of the line, dealt by the threat next to each (`FrontSolver`, `military.json` fronts); several armies on a front each hold a contiguous sector, sized by their divisions and taken in the order they stand; a front's cohesion limits how far a division is sent to fill a gap, and a hand order pins a stack until it arrives (docs/FRONTLINES.md). A front in the nation's own ground with no enemy next to it is a fallback line (`Front.is_fallback`): stacks on it are `prepared` and dig deeper and faster; a garrison front (`kind`) deals divisions to the provinces it guards, the nearest first. Attack plans (`Offensive`, run by `Offensives.step_day`, constants in `military.json` offensive) are broad pushes or spearheads with phase lines; stacks standing on their front with a plan build `planning` (a bonus to attacking power), and the plan's aggressiveness sets the odds and morale at which they attack. The player gives these orders with the Battle Plans toolbar (`BattlePlanToolbar`, over the command panel): one tool at a time takes the map's clicks and drags (MapView routes them through the `map_tool` group); a border front can hold only the stretch around a clicked province (`Front.anchor`, `Military.border_stretch`); drawn lines and target lines are reshaped with `SetFrontLine` and `SetOffensive` "targets"; an invasion plan gathers its army in a port and sails it with `SendOverseas`; warships on the Shore support mission add `Military.naval_support` to attacks on the coast they lie off. The AI's fronts still give each army one province. Ships are squadrons in fleets that control sea zones, blockade and carry armies. Provinces have a controller apart from the owner. An attacking division that enters a province with no defender takes it at once; contested ground (where a battle was fought lately) still takes a few days to mop up (`occupation.mop_up_days`). A railway at home speeds marching on one's own land (`home_rail`).

### Diplomacy (`diplomacy.gd`, `peace.gd`, `data/diplomacy.json`)
Relations between every pair; treaties (alliance, guarantee, non-aggression, passage, neutrality pact) with an entente grade; casus belli and claims; war declarations that cost diplomacy points and prestige; mobilization as a diplomatic act that alarms neighbours; calls to arms that put a join decision to the player; peace priced in war score, with provinces, indemnities and disarmament as terms. Ceded provinces must touch the taker's land, through each other or across a sea it holds or has a coast on, and a capital goes only with the whole nation (`Peace.province_problem`); a great power gives up at most a quarter of its provinces in one treaty (`Peace.max_cession`, `peace.great_power_max_share`). Ground ceded at a peace or to a demand leaves the loser a reconquest claim to it for `revanche.years` (`GameState.claims`, saved; `Diplomacy.revanche`), gone once it has it back, and a rivalry with the winner (a floor under its grievance) that fades over the years: the claim is a casus belli and the first thing it asks in a crisis. A demand may also be a fleet limit (no new warships for some years, `Peace.fleet_limited`). A nation is beaten (`Peace.is_forced`) when its war support is gone, or the taker holds its capital and its support is at the floor, or the taker occupies most of it; it then gives up to `peace.forced.budget_mult` times the taker's score, but only the ground the taker holds, the war's goal and money. War support (0 to 100) falls with casualties and blockade and decides when a nation will take any peace.

### Historical pressure (`pressure.gd`, `data/events.json`)
- **Tension**, 0 to 100, is made of a base, the great powers' arms race (their men under arms over a base that, once two great powers have fought a war to its end, follows the armies by `arms_drift` a month, so a lasting army becomes the norm, a new race shows and armies sent home ease it), mobilization, wars between them, rivalry (and two rival blocs), and the lasting force of events, which fades. It shows as a flame in the top bar and on the Overview.
- In a `pressure_ai` scenario the AI starts no war of its own choosing below the war gate (`Pressure.war_gate`: 30, or what the scenario sets; the 1900 game's gate falls from 48 in 1900 to 35 by 1912, so the long game does not reach for war in its first years), and above it grows bolder with tension; above 50 the great powers' quarrels worsen each month.
- **Rivalries**: a scenario's old quarrels (France and Germany over Alsace-Lorraine, Serbia and Austria-Hungary, Russia and Austria-Hungary, the Balkan states and the Ottomans) are a floor under a nation's grievance that fades a point a year (`gs.rivalries`), so rising tension feeds them rather than the weakest neighbour. The 1900 nations have temperaments of their own (`ai_traits`, from `tools/make_1900.py`): the Balkan states bold, the great powers between, the neutrals too timid to form ambitions.
- **Neutrals**: a nation in no bloc, allied to no great power and not mobilized (`Diplomacy.is_neutral`) is weighed heavily against as a target (`ai.json` diplomacy.war.neutral_penalty, guaranteed_penalty, ambition.w_neutral), and a war on one with no casus belli costs the aggressor more in the world's eyes, prestige and tension (`diplomacy.json` war.neutral_violation).
- **Events**: from the Boxer Rising (1900) to the Liman von Sanders mission (1913) and Sarajevo (1914), at least two for every nation (`validate_data.py` checks it), and three that put a choice to a small nation the player leads every year or two (a great power courting it, a trade treaty, an incident on the border; `player_minor`, picks `$suitor`, `$trade_partner`, `$great_neighbour`). Each fires on its date while its conditions hold. If it concerns the player, time stops and each answer is a button with its effects; otherwise the AI's choice is made and it is news. Effects: tension, relations, treasury (or `revenue`: months of the nation's revenue, so it scales with the nation), prestige, war support, diplomacy points, manpower, mobilization, grievance, treaties, wars. Events dated before a scenario's start are history already. Three come from the state of the world rather than the calendar, and pick their nations by what they are now (`tension.picks`, `Pressure._pick_role`): the lost provinces (a nation that gave up ground at a peace in the last two years, against the winner), unrest in an empire (Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire or Russia, at peace with low war support or after a loss) and a conference on armaments (the two most armed great powers, after a great-power war, when tension is up and no great powers fight); none fires in a scenario's first year.
- **Public stands**: from the Overview any nation may take one stand every half year, for diplomacy points: an appeal for calm lowers tension by as much as the nation weighs (a base, more for a great power and for prestige, more the tenser Europe is, at most `calm.tension_max`: a great power's at high tension does what the old conference of the powers did; `Pressure.appeal_tension`), or it rattles the sabre (tension up, prestige and war support, and every land neighbour thinks less of it).
- **Ambitions** (`scripts/sim/ambition.gd`, `data/ambitions.json`): the player may set the nation a direction on the Overview (keep out of the great war, unify the nation, become a great power), offered where it fits; `Ambition.status` says each month how it stands and the History book records when it is set, achieved or lost. A direction, not a score.
- **No ending**: the game is an open sandbox. Nothing stops the clock; a general war, a peace or a long quiet are all just where the years lead.
- Nothing is guaranteed: left alone, the 1900 world drifts to a general war in 1914; a player who cools things down or hardens them changes that.

### Dynamic crises (`scripts/sim/crisis.gd`, `data/crises.json`)
The authored calendar of pressure ends around Sarajevo; crises are generated on the monthly path for any scenario with `pressure_ai`. Each month the quarrels of the world are scored (how much the target dislikes the demander, grievance, a claim, an alarm, tension, and the demander's edge); a pair may quarrel only if the demander's side (allies and guarantors with it) has `gate.min_odds` of the other side's strength, and an old scenario claim is pressed only from tension `gate.claim_min_tension` (ground lost at a peace in living memory at any tension); the best pair at or above `gate.min_score` opens a crisis, at most one at a time per nation, two at once, one every few months, and a pair rests for years. The demander (never the player) asks for ground it lost at a peace (revanche), a claim's provinces, a border province, an indemnity, the withdrawal of border armies, or, between great powers with fleets, no new warships for some years (naval); the demand is a peace demand and the goal of a war over it (see war goals). The nation asked has until the deadline: it yields (the demand is carried out), refuses (the demander gets a casus belli with the goal, and for `war.provocation.refusal_days` the AI's desire for that war gains `w_refused`; against a nation holding ground it lost it gains `w_revanche`, and ground lost at the peace after a war counts as provocation: the loser may go to war for it in a calm Europe, unmobilized), mobilizes (a demander that can no longer win backs down) or appeals to the powers. The AI answers after `think_days` from the odds of the two sides (with allies, guarantors and backers), its caution, the price of the demand and the chance of a mediator; the player is asked at once as an event, and left unanswered past the deadline it counts as a refusal. Demands are how a war gets its reason (there is no separate justification): the player opens one from the diplomacy panel (Make a demand, the goal picker: named provinces or a concession, `PressDemand`, `Crisis.press`, for `press.cost_points` times the goal's `cost_mult`, less what prestige buys), and an AI nation with a plan against a neighbour but no casus belli makes one (`AIDiplomacy.consider_press`); the nation asked answers like any other, and a refusal leaves the demander a casus belli with the demand as its goal. A third nation (the player too, from the diplomacy panel) may mediate, once in a crisis (a chance from its prestige and goodwill with both; success ends it with a modest indemnity), or back a side. Finished crises are kept in `GameState.crisis_log`. `tools/dynamic_crisis_report.gd` lists the crises of an AI-only run, for tuning.

### Difficulty and coalitions (`scripts/sim/difficulty.gd`, `scripts/ai/ai_coalition.gd`)
`data/difficulty.json` lists presets as multipliers on dotted paths into `data/ai.json` and `data/crises.json`; `Difficulty.apply` reloads both files and scales them (so it is idempotent and Historical gives the shipped numbers). `GameState.difficulty` is chosen on the New game page, saved with the game and re-applied on load; a save from before difficulties plays as Historical. Coalitions (`data/ai.json`, `coalition`): `AICoalition.claimed` costs an offence utility when an ally already aims a campaign at the same province; `hold` (off in Historical, which was calibrated without it, on in Challenging) keeps a nation in a war while allies fight on, unless it is `strained` (war support, a lost capital, a bad war score); and in the monthly overtures a nation that wants terms offers them as a joint peace (a `Conference`) when every ally at war with the enemy is an AI nation that wants peace too.

### Battle staff report (`Military.battle_forecast`)
`battle_forecast` works out a day's fighting in a province the way `_fight` does, changing nothing: each side's men, daily loss, the loss as a share of its men and the days to half strength, with optional "what if" tweaks (a side's fighting power, extra trench levels). The battle screen's `staff_report` reads it for a battle the player is in and offers three what-ifs. Event options carry an advisor line (`Pressure.advisor_line`: the one effect that matters most, a war, a treaty, then the largest figure by weight).

### Staff tools (`Military.order_preview`, the Supply variant of Military mode)
`Military.order_preview` works out what an order would do before it is given: the road (`find_path`), its days and the date of arrival, the stages out of supply (`supply_cost` is infinite there), and the enemy at the destination as far as `Intel` allows: only armies the player sees as estimates are counted, as the range the estimate permits (never the true men), with the odds on men alone. The map shows it as a tip when armies are selected (`MapView.order_tip_for`); the Supply variant colours every province by `supply_cost` for the player. An order given by hand takes an army off its front (`Military.leave_front`), so handing armies to a front never outranks an order.

### Peace conferences (`scripts/sim/conference.gd`)
Peace stays pairwise (one war between two nations), but nations on one side of a conflict can make peace with the same enemy together. `Conference.partners` finds the allies at war with the enemy; each asks (`AIPeace.price_terms`) for what its own goal and war score pay for. The package is offered whole: an AI enemy takes it only if it would take every entry, the cost of all of them together is within its strongest wish for peace, and it can carry every indemnity together; a refusal may produce a counter-offer (`Conference.counter` strikes the dearest demand, goal demands last, until the enemy would take it), kept in `GameState.conference_counter` for the player to accept or refuse within `peace.counter_days`, and put to the enemy again when accepted. An AI nation whose table cannot be set (a partner's terms cannot be made) proposes alone. The partner with the highest war score chairs and gains `peace.conference_prestige`; while the conference makes its peaces `Peace.joint_tags` stops them counting as separate peaces. A player enemy gets the entries as ordinary offers, one by one. The peace screen shows the table (checkboxes per partner) and the counter-offer.

### The history of the run (`scripts/sim/history.gd`, `scripts/ui/history_book.gd`)
Three records in `GameState`: `history_log` (wars, peaces with their terms, events, crises, treaties, collapses, in order; capped), `territory_log` (every change of owner, from `transfer_province`) and `history_months` (a row of figures each month: wars, battles and men lost since the last row, provinces that changed hands, tension, the leading industries). `History` builds the sections from them (a year's summary, a headline for each month from its row and the seed, the wars, the territory lines, the nations against the start, a low-resolution map of who owns what) and writes the whole book as Markdown or HTML (`user://history/`). The History tab of the Nation screen (H) shows it and exports it. Nothing in the simulation reads these records.

### Briefing and explanations (`scripts/sim/briefing.gd`)
Read-only. `Briefing.issues` ranks what a nation should look at (money, supply, war, neighbours, tension, idle effort) with a reason and the tab to open; the Overview shows the top three. `AIDiplomacy.war_check` is the single weighing of a declaration (`war_option` and `explain_declare` both read it) and `AIPeace.urge_parts` the parts of the wish for peace (`urge` and `explain` read them), so the lines in the diplomacy panel and the peace screen come from the numbers the AI acts on.

### AI (`scripts/ai/`, `data/ai.json`)
Every other nation plays by the same rules. **Nothing in a scenario scripts what the AI does**: there are no authored war plans and no army-quality bonuses; wars, fronts and campaigns arise from the modules weighing their options (utility scoring over odds, distance, value of the target, the nation's temperament and a small per-game noise, `gs.ai_seed`, so no two games go alike).

- **Modules:** `ai_budget` (taxes, state investment, the arms share and munitions bought abroad, one building a month), `ai_forces` (raising units; mix and training cap; sending men home after a war), `ai_defense` (anchors per front, rebalancing by proximity, settling), `ai_offense` (several campaigns at once, each with a gather point; odds against the enemy decide the start, a fall-back morale decides the retreat), `ai_landing` and `ai_navy`, `ai_diplomacy` (treaties, declarations, demands), `ai_peace` (seeking and accepting peace from war score, war support and how long the fighting has been quiet; indemnity offers), `ai_ambition`.
- **Numbers:** every weight and threshold is in `data/ai.json` (read as `AI.cfg`). `AI.overrides` lets a game give each nation its own copy, which the trainer uses.
- **Training:** `tools/ai_train.py` tunes 67 of those numbers by self-play (an evolution strategy, rank-weighted, mirrored sampling). `tools/ai_arena.gd` plays one game of two groups of nations (candidate against baseline, then swapped), 400 days from July 1914 with the blocs at war; fitness is the change in the group's share of Europe's people and output (times 100) and of its provinces (times 50), plus 5 times the change in its share of soldiers, less 0.2 for each month a nation is at war. `run N` trains from `tools/ai_training/state.json` (a fresh start when there is none), `test N` plays the trained numbers against the starting ones on fresh seeds, `apply` writes them into `data/ai.json`. The shipped numbers are from the first round (`round1/`): 40 generations that beat the originals in all 16 unseen seeds (mean +11.3); that fitness did not reward ending wars, so the great war ran for years. A second round (`round2/`), under the war goals, crises and conferences, did not beat them clearly (mean +1.3, standard error 1.2, 10 of 16) and was not applied. A third (`round3/`, step 7 of PLAN.md), under the rules of steps 1 to 6 with the fitness above and step 3's arming, buying, investing and demobilizing numbers added, beat the shipped numbers in all 16 unseen seeds (mean +8.0, standard error 0.9), but most of that came from making peace sooner (the fighting numbers alone: +3.7, 11 of 16), and in the campaign checks it made things worse: from 1900 France and Germany fought 6 months instead of 9.75 and only 1 seed in 10 ran short of shells in the general war's first half year (5 with the shipped numbers); from July 1914 five provinces with no land link changed hands (none). The peace-time numbers (arming in peace, investment, sending men home) get no signal in an arena that is all war. So it was declined: the shipped numbers stay those of the first round.

## Interface

- **Top bar:** nation, treasury and its monthly change, recruits (while mobilized or at war), diplomacy points, tension, the Nation button, date, pause and speed, settings.
- **Map:** map modes (1 to 6) with a strip for the variants and a legend, the province card, army and fleet banners, the command panel (C) for orders, the build queue, the news feed.
- **Nation screen (N):** one window with tabs: Overview (the cabinet briefing, Europe's tension with its breakdown and chart, recent events), Economy (E), Build (B), Military (M), Navy (Y), Diplomacy (F), Focus (O), Rankings (R), History (H). A right click on your own land opens it; on another nation it opens the country panel.
- **Decisions** stop time: join prompts, peace offers and events.
- **Pause menu (Esc):** resume, save and load, settings, main menu, and the list of keys. **Tutorial:** fourteen steps in 1900.

## Map and data

942 provinces on a 7857 by 5152 bitmap (`data/map/provinces.png`, one flat colour each; `provinces.tsv` has owner, area and centre), with generated adjacencies, coasts, ports, sea zones and borders. A shader paints ownership from a lookup texture, so a change of owner writes a few pixels. Land texture (satellite or painted), relief, and the province bitmap's smaller copies for far zooms are generated by scripts in `tools/`. 101 cities per scenario with about 40 famous landmarks (`data/landmarks.json`). The 3D map draws the flat map on a tilted plane raised by relief, with city models, unit models and cards that match the 2D banners, shader smoke and a quality setting.

## Architecture

- `GameState` (autoload) holds every fact; the map and UI only read it. `Clock` ticks days and months. All changes go through commands (`GameState.issue`), which is what makes saves, tests and the AI share one path.
- The running game does a day's step a stage a frame; tests and tools run all stages at once.
- Saves are the whole state as compressed variant bytes; derived tables are rebuilt on load. A save carries a format version (`SaveGame.VERSION`, currently 3); versions from `OLDEST` up are read, older steps are brought forward by `_migrate`, and newer or older-than-oldest saves are refused with a message.
- `Economy`, `Military`, `Navy`, `Diplomacy`, `Pressure`, `Focus` are static classes over that state; their numbers are in `data/`.
- Folders: `scripts/sim`, `scripts/ai`, `scripts/map`, `scripts/ui`, `data`, `tools`, `tests`.

## Testing and balance

Headless tests under `tests/` (`tools/run_tests.py`), with windowed ones under `xvfb`. `tools/balance_run.gd` prints a yearly table of the great powers (output, treasury, balance, people, armies, land), tension and wars for a long AI-only run of a scenario; used for the balance pass. `tools/soak.gd` and `tools/ui_soak.gd` fuzz the simulation and the interface. ## Balance notes (the 1900 scenario)

The AI's economies grow about 4% a year (development, people and one building a month); a player who builds and invests grows 6 to 8%, so the rankings move: from 1900, Italy building and investing is the largest economy by about 1940, Serbia with a 15% tax, buildings and investment climbs from 19th to 13th by 1950, and Luxembourg from 20th to 15th (`tools/economy.py --policies ai,ai+invest,ai+all+invest,ai+tax=0.15+all+invest --years 50`). A player who does nothing falls behind. Tension rises from the mid-20s to about 60 by 1912, the Balkan League's war comes in 1912 and the general war in 1914 in most runs; after that the world the nations have built decides.

## Sound

The `Audio` autoload (`scripts/audio.gd`, `data/audio.json`) has buses Master, Music, Effects and Interface and plays six pieces (`data/audio/music/`): *Diplomatic Silence* (the menu theme and peace), *Ein letzter Tanzschritt* (peace), *Marche Impériale* (peace and war), and *Diplomacy of Steel*, *Mechanical March* and *Brandschaftsmarsch* (war). Music follows the game (menu, peace, or war when the player's nation is fighting) with a crossfade, or the player picks a piece, pauses, skips or repeats. Volume, mute and the music settings are saved with the UI settings; they sit under Settings in the main menu and in the Settings window, and the pause menu has a compact player.

## Not in the game

Multiplayer; scripted outcomes; a score; land outside Europe (the Ottoman Empire and Russia are cut by the map's frame); technology.
