# Foreign Skies: Design Plan

## Vision
Age of History 3's accessibility (flat 2D map, quick to learn, any nation playable) with Paradox-style depth in diplomacy, economy and war. The map is the interface: map modes carry most of the information.

## Locked decisions
| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Setting | Real world, stylized but semi-realistic map, realistic data |
| Time | Real time with pause, 5 speeds, day-based clock |
| First playable | Western Europe, WW1 scenario (start: July 1914). Basic military, political and economic systems. No scripted events. |
| Map | Hand-drawn province bitmap (EU4/CK3 style) |
| Multiplayer | None (single-player only) |

## Scenario: July 1914, Western Europe
**Playable/major:** United Kingdom, France, German Empire, Belgium, Netherlands, Luxembourg, Switzerland, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Denmark.
**Edge stubs (AI-driven, partly on-map):** Austria-Hungary, Russia. Their fronts are abstracted so the Western theatre is complete.
**Blocs:** Entente (UK, France, Russia) vs Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary). Italy starts neutral.
**Systemic (non-scripted) triggers:** alliance obligations, guarantees (UK guarantees Belgium), mobilization, and war declarations. The war should be able to start, or not start, from system rules alone.

## Core systems (v1)

### Political
- Government type and war support (0-100). War support drops with casualties and blockade and rises with victories and defense of home soil.
- Diplomacy: relations, alliances, guarantees, non-aggression pacts, war declarations with a casus belli, and peace deals priced in war score.
- Neutrality: nations may stay out. Violating a guaranteed nation's borders triggers the guarantor's join decision.

### Economic
- Province output: manpower, industry, food, gold from population and development.
- Nation treasury, monthly budget (tax rate, army upkeep, spending on building and recruiting).
- Mobilization level scales manpower pool against economic output, so mobilizing is costly.
- Blockade: naval control of sea zones cuts trade income and food.

### Military
- Land units: infantry, cavalry, artillery, grouped into armies with a general.
- Combat uses strength, terrain, entrenchment, and morale. Entrenchment makes defense strong, which produces WW1-style stalemates.
- Supply: armies need supply from owned or occupied provinces, and range limits offensives.
- Sieges and occupation: provinces have a controller separate from the owner.
- Navy (basic): fleets control sea zones, escort or block transport.

### AI
- Utility-scored goals each tick: defend, attack, mobilize, ally, make peace.
- Per-nation traits (aggression, caution). A war plan layer, for example France's offensive plan and Germany's Schlieffen-style opening, expressed as data weights rather than scripts.

## Map and data pipeline
This is the biggest piece of work.

**Files (all under `res://data/map/`)**
- `provinces.png`: each province is a unique flat RGB color. The source of truth, and hand-editable in any image editor.
- `provinces.tsv`: `id, r, g, b, name` (tab-separated).
- `terrain.png`: terrain type per pixel (or per province in `province_terrain.csv`).
- `rivers.png`, `heightmap.png`: optional visual and combat modifiers.
- `adjacencies.tsv`: auto-generated from `provinces.png` by an editor tool, plus manual overrides (straits, sea crossings).
- `sea_zones`: provinces flagged as sea.

**Rendering:** a shader samples `provinces.png` and a lookup texture (province id to owner color) to paint political color, borders and selection. Updating ownership means writing a few pixels in the lookup texture, not redrawing the map.

**Scale:** 878 provinces from the Atlantic to the Urals, 7857x5152 bitmap (see Roadmap phase 5, step 0).

**Real data sources:** Natural Earth (boundaries, rivers, terrain), historical 1914 border references, census and population figures for 1910-1914, order-of-battle references for 1914 armies.

## Architecture (Godot 4.7)
- Simulation separate from presentation: `GameState` holds all data, and the map and UI only read it.
- `Clock` autoload emitting `day_tick`, `month_tick`, `year_tick`.
- Command pattern for all player and AI actions (save/load and testing for free).
- Content as data: nations, scenarios, units and costs in JSON/`.tres`, with no hard-coded values.
- Planned layout:
  - `scripts/sim/` simulation (state, economy, military, diplomacy, AI)
  - `scripts/map/` rendering, selection, map modes, camera
  - `scripts/ui/` HUD, panels
  - `data/` map bitmaps, nations, scenarios
  - `tools/` one-off scripts (adjacency builder, map validator)

## Roadmap
Status: phases 1 to 12 are built (phase 6, the AI, step by step below). Phases 13 and 14 are proposed; phase 15 (a second economic rework) is in progress.

1. **Map foundation.** *(done)* Province bitmap, shader rendering, selection, camera, adjacency builder, map modes (political, terrain).
2. **Sim core.** *(done)* Clock, `GameState`, nation and province data, the 1914 scenario loader, and the top bar HUD.
3. **Economy.** *(done)* `Economy` (scripts/sim/economy.gd) derives per-province GDP, industry, food and recruits from population density, the owner's development, province buildings and national programs, then each nation's monthly budget: tax revenue (with a drag above the default rate), civil, army, navy, food-import and program costs. Growth comes from a baseline rate plus the Education program. **Buildings** (farmland, factory, railway, port, trading house; up to 3 levels) are paid up front, take days to build, and are built from the Construction tab for the selected province; construction is lost if the province changes hands. Ports need a coastal province (`data/map/coastal.tsv`, from `tools/build_coastal.py`). Ports and trading houses create trade income, which a blockade removes. **Resources** (coal, iron ore, timber; thousand tonnes per year) have a per-province deposit (`data/scenarios/july_1914/resources.tsv`, built by `tools/build_resources.py` from 1913 geography and national output). Coal mines, iron mines and sawmills (level 2 at the start) set how much of a deposit is extracted. Each nation balances production against demand from industry, households and its own buildings (factories and railways burn coal and iron): shortfalls are imported and surpluses exported, with the state taking half the trade value, and a stockpile buffers a blockade. When a blockade has drained the stockpile, shortages cut industry and output. Buildings need materials, drawn from the stockpile with any shortfall bought at a markup (and impossible under blockade), so resource-poor nations build dearer. The Resources tab shows each balance. The Construction tab can **mass build** one level of any building in many provinces at once (`MassConstructBuilding`): best provinces first (biggest deposit for mines, most output otherwise), up to a province limit and a spending limit, stopping when money runs out, with an exact preview. The Buildings tab shows what each type earns and produces, and a queue with progress bars is always on screen at bottom-left (click a row to jump to the province). **Programs** (agricultural subsidies, industrial subsidies, education) are switched on and off for a running cost. **Mobilization** lives in the Military panel (M): it trades output for manpower. Food shortage and `Nation.blockade` (set by the navy in phase 7) cut output. All changes go through commands (`SetEconomyPolicy`, `ConstructBuilding`, `SetProgram`) via `GameState.issue()`. Constants and building/program definitions are in `data/economy.json`; per-nation `development` and `agri_yield` are calibrated in the scenario. Economy panel: E. Tests: `tests/economy_test.gd`.
   *Map modes:* buttons at the bottom right (and keys 1-6) switch between Political, Resources (coal, iron or timber deposits), Income (tax per province), Population, Buildings (levels of all buildings or of one type) and Provinces (debug colours). Modes with a choice show a submode picker, and shaded modes show a legend; pressing a mode's key again steps through its submodes. Dynamic modes (Income, Buildings) repaint when the economy changes. The camera ignores the scroll wheel while the cursor is over any UI control, because Godot lets wheel events through even stop-filtered panels; `tests/ui_input_test.gd` checks this in a real window.
   *Economy UI conventions:* every figure is per month (money in GBP millions, resource flows in kt/Mt; stockpiles are plain amounts) and comes from `Fmt` (scripts/ui/fmt.gd). The Economy panel (E) has three tabs: **Overview** (problems list, tax rate, grouped income and spending, national programs), **Resources** (food, coal, iron, timber: made, used, balance, stock), **Buildings** (one table of every building type with a Build button for the selected province; mass build folded away). Detail lives in tooltips. Food imports cost the treasury only a tenth of their value (`food_state_share`) because the whole economy buys food.
4. **Military.** *(done)* Divisions grouped into armies that march province to province, fight, dig in, draw supply and occupy. Rules are in `Military` (scripts/sim/military.gd), constants in `data/military.json`, units in `Unit`/`Army`. Tests: `tests/military_test.gd` (sim) and `tests/military_ui_test.gd` (UI, headless).
   *Units:* infantry division (12,000 men), cavalry division (4,000), artillery brigade (3,000). A unit has strength (share of its full size) and morale. Armies hold units and a general (a power multiplier). 90% of each nation's `peacetime_army` starts in the field, placed by `deployment` in the scenario file (garrisons and frontier districts, dealt out by weight); the other 10% is the recruit reserve (`Nation.manpower`). `Nation.total_men()` is reserve plus men under arms and in training, and is what upkeep and the mobilization cap apply to. **Recruiting** (`RaiseUnits`) pays recruits and money up front, takes training days, then the unit joins an army in that province.
   *Terrain:* `data/map/terrain.tsv` (from `tools/build_terrain.py`, hand-curated per province since no elevation data is bundled): plains, forest, hills, mountains, marsh, urban. It sets defence, movement cost and siege time.
   *Movement:* province to province along land borders and strait links (open-sea links wait for the navy). Days per step come from centre distance, the slowest unit's speed, destination terrain, and railways in provinces you hold. Armies may only enter their own ground, ground they hold, or ground of a nation they are at war with, so neutral borders are closed until diplomacy adds passage rights. Orders (`MoveArmy`) take the cheapest path.
   *Combat:* every day, hostile armies in one province fight. Each side's power (men in thousands times type attack, dulled by low morale, times the general) becomes enemy casualties, about 1% of the men a day for equal armies. The army that was there first defends: terrain defence times entrenchment (+12% a level, up to 5 levels, one level per 4 days standing still; moving resets it) divides its casualties. Casualties wear morale down; below 25% the army routs to the best neighbouring province (10% extra loss) or is lost if it has none.
   *Supply:* an army is supplied within 3 hops of ground its nation holds (a railway hop counts half). Supplied armies recover morale and refill from the reserve; unsupplied ones lose strength and morale daily.
   *Occupation:* an army alone in a province held by a nation it is at war with takes control after a siege (terrain's `occupy_days`; the province's owner does not change). The occupier collects a quarter of the province's GDP as tax; the owner loses its output and recruits, but its people still need feeding. An army of the owner retakes it the same way.
   *Routs and collapse:* a beaten army only falls back into a neighbouring province its nation still holds; with none it is cut off and lost, so a defeated army cannot slip onto its own occupied land and retake it for free. A nation that holds no province at all loses every army and all training at once (`GameState.nation_collapsed`).
   *War state and records:* `GameState.wars` (active `War` objects, keyed by nation pair) with `at_war()`, `war_between()`, `wars_of()` and `war_history`; `SetWar` (the Military panel's War section) starts and ends wars for testing until phase 5 replaces it. A `War` (scripts/sim/war.gd) records who started it and when, each side's losses in battle, to attrition and cut off or disbanded, provinces and people taken, every finished battle (place, dates, attacker, winner, starting strengths, losses) and a dated log of battles, captures, surrenders, collapses, declarations and peace. Battles are tracked per province (`GameState.engagements`) and written into the war when no hostile armies share the province any more; the winner is the one side left standing. The Military panel's **Wars** tab lists active wars (yours first) and past ones, with a table of both sides' results and a filterable log; double-click an entry to jump to where it happened.
   *UI:* army banners on the map (men in thousands, red outline while fighting, yellow when selected, selected armies' routes drawn). Clicking a province selects your armies there and, if it holds several armies, fans them out as individual named banners (all nations' armies, yours first): click one banner to select just that army, Shift-click to add or remove one. Under the name of every fanned-out army, and under any selected army, small unit symbols with counts show its composition (`Army.composition`). Right-click another province to march. The Military panel (M) lists armies with merge, split in half and split off by unit type (pick infantry, cavalry or artillery and how many; at least one unit stays behind), recruiting, and the war switch. Map modes 7 (Terrain) and 8 (Military: who holds each province, occupied ones darkened). An event feed at the top centre reports battles, captures, wars and new units.
   *Selection and symbols:* dragging a box on the map selects every one of your armies inside it (Shift adds to the selection). Banners show a NATO-style symbol (infantry, cavalry, artillery, or one the player sets: guard, fortress garrison, reserve; `SetArmyIcon`), the men in thousands, and a size mark (X, XX, XXX) from `size_marks` in `data/military.json`.
   *Route times on the map:* the selected armies' routes show the days to reach each province and the arrival date; other marching armies show their destination; hovering a province with armies selected previews the route and its time before you right-click.
   *Fronts:* a front (`Front`, `DesignateFront`, `AssignToFront`, `DeleteFront`) is the set of provinces to hold. The usual way to make one is "Front vs nation" in the Military panel: click a province of the neighbouring nation and the front is every province you hold that touches ground that nation holds (`Military.border_provinces`). It follows the war: the line is re-read daily, so conquests and losses move it. "Draw line" makes a hand-drawn chain of bordering provinces instead. Armies assigned to a front are dealt out along it by strength (`Military.step_fronts`), walk to their province and dig in; a manual order releases an army from its front, and destroyed armies drop off it. Fronts are drawn on the map with their names and army counts.
   *Selection:* click a province to select your armies there; click it again, click ground without your armies, press Esc, or use the Deselect button to clear the selection.
   *Occupation on the map:* the Political mode stripes an occupied province with the occupier's colour over the owner's (shader `occupation_stripes`); the Military mode paints it solid in the occupier's colour.
   *Battle screen:* every battle keeps a full account (`Military._engagement`, read with `GameState.battle_by_id` / `battle_at`): terrain, dates, attacker and winner; per side the armies, generals, starting and current or final composition, men, morale and the defender's terrain-and-trench multiplier; a day-by-day timeline of men, losses and morale; and events (who joined, who routed or was cut off, who held the field). Battles in progress are in `GameState.engagements`; finished ones go to `battle_archive` (the newest 300), and the war record points at them by id. While a battle is on, a red crossed-swords icon with the day count sits beside the province's banners on the map; clicking it, or double-clicking a battle in the Wars tab's Battles list (or in its log), opens the battle screen (`BattleScreen`, with `BattleChart` for the strength and morale lines). An open screen updates daily for a battle in progress and shows the result once it ends.
   *Not yet:* alliances and passage rights (phase 5), AI-driven armies (phase 6), sea transport and naval supply (phase 7), war score from occupation (phase 5).
5. **Diplomacy and war.** *(done, steps 0 to 8 below)* Relations, alliances, guarantees, war declarations, peace deals.

   **Starting point (what phase 4 left us).** `GameState.wars` is pairwise (`A|B` keys), `SetWar` is a testing stand-in, and `Military.can_enter` only allows own ground, held ground or an enemy's. `scenario.json` already carries `blocs`, `neutral_pacts` and `guarantees` data that nothing reads yet. There is no Russia, and no war score, war support or casus belli. `Nation.blockade` is still a placeholder for phase 7.

   **Design decisions.**
   - Wars stay pairwise (`War` is one record per enemy pair). An alliance war is several pairwise wars opened together, so combat, occupation and the war record need no rewrite. A shared `war_group` id on each `War` ties the pairs of one conflict, and the peace screen groups them by it.
   - A nation can make a separate peace; allies are not dragged along. Allies who stop fighting lose trust with the rest of the bloc (see Relations).
   - Everything goes through commands. Diplomacy state lives in a new `Diplomacy` rules class (static, like `Economy` and `Military`) acting on `GameState`; `DiplomacyState` data sits in `GameState` so save/load needs nothing new.
   - Constants and definitions in `data/diplomacy.json`; the starting web of treaties comes from the scenario file.

   **Data model.**
   - `GameState.relations`: sorted pair key to opinion, -100..100 (starts from the scenario, drifts toward a baseline).
   - `GameState.treaties`: array of `Treaty` `{id, kind, a, b, start_day, end_day}` with kinds `alliance`, `guarantee` (one way: a guarantees b), `nap` (non-aggression), `passage` (one way: a lets b's armies cross), `neutral_pact` (Italy's Triple Alliance opt-out clause).
   - `Nation` gains `war_support` (0-100), `prestige`, `stability` is left out of v1. `War` gains `war_group`, `casus_belli`, `score` (per side, see below) and `occupied_share`.
   - `GameState.mobilization_calls`: tags that have been called up by an alliance this crisis, used for the join rules.

   **Systems, in build order.** Each step ends with its own tests and is playable on its own.
   0. **Map expansion.** *(done)* The map now runs from the Atlantic to the Urals (lon -11 to 61, lat 34.8 to 67) and covers the whole Balkans: 7857x5152 px, 878 provinces (was 3328x3712, 556). Existing province ids, colours and names did not change; new provinces were appended (ids 557-717 Balkans, 718-878 Russia). Built by `tools/extend_map.py <natural-earth admin-1> <slice>`, which pastes the old bitmap into the larger canvas and paints one slice at a time (`balkans`, then `russia`); it also extends provinces the old frame had cut (East Prussia, Slovakia, Hungary, Scotland, Crete's strip) and writes an `admin` column (modern country) into `provinces.tsv` for the new rows. Slice definitions (owners by country and unit, 1914 border fixes, renames, merge/split limits) are the `SLICES` dict in that script. After a slice: `build_adjacencies.py`, `build_coastal.py`, `build_terrain.py`, `build_population.py <populated places>`, `build_resources.py`, then recalibrate (`tools/calibrate_nations.gd`, see below). The population and resource builders keep a separate national total per slice (`GROUPS`, ids by range), so adding a region never changes the numbers of an older one.
      *New nations (tags in `data/nations.json`):* SRB, MNE, ALB, GRE, BUL, ROM, OTT (European Thrace and Constantinople only) and RUS (European Russia, the Urals, the North Caucasus, Finland, the Baltic provinces, Poland east of the Prussian partition, Belarus, Ukraine without Galicia, Moldova). Austria-Hungary now includes Galicia, Bukovina, Carpathian Ruthenia, Transylvania, the Banat and Bačka, and all of Hungary. Kaliningrad and Memel belong to Germany. Transcaucasia, Anatolia, Kazakhstan, Norway and Sweden are grey background land (not playable). The map is cut at lat 67 and lon 61, so Lapland, Murmansk and the Arctic are truncated.
      *Scenario data for the new nations:* development, agri_yield, intensity, army, treasury, deployments and generals are in `scenario.json` and `military.json`. Each nation's economy is calibrated by `tools/calibrate_nations.gd` (prints GDP per head, revenue, food balance, resource demand and net budget for every nation; run `godot --headless --path . -s res://tools/calibrate_nations.gd`). Targets: GDP per head about 12-22 GBP for the new nations (relative to Italy 28), revenue 150 for Austria-Hungary and 280 for Germany (as `economy_test.gd` checks), peacetime armies sized so no nation starts in deficit (a small nation cannot afford its historical army at 70 GBP a man a year, so mobilization has to supply the rest).
      *Known simplifications:* the scenario `area` column is equal-area pixels (scaled by cos(latitude) against the 47 deg of the original frame), so densities stay comparable; the old west still has two oddities carried over from the first frame (Polish units outside the Prussian partition were once labelled German; only Lodz 1 and 2 were corrected to Russian). Province borders follow modern administrative units; Russian provinces are modern oblasts named after the governorate they roughly match. Iron ore, coal and timber for the new regions are rough national totals spread by weight.
   1. **Foundations and data.** *(done)* `Diplomacy` (scripts/sim/diplomacy.gd, static rules), `Treaty` (scripts/sim/treaty.gd) and `data/diplomacy.json` exist. `GameState` holds `relations`, `relation_base`, `treaties` and `mobilization_calls`, with signals `treaty_changed` and `relations_changed`; opinions drift monthly toward a baseline built from blocs and treaty bonuses. The scenario now seeds `alliances` (terms `defensive_only` or `entente`), `neutral_pacts` (Italy: a defensive-only alliance plus the neutrality clause), `guarantees`, `passage`, `nap`, `relations` overrides and `starting_wars` (Austria-Hungary vs Serbia is already on at the start). Queries: `are_allied`, `guarantors_of`, `has_passage`, `find_treaty`, `add_treaty`, `end_treaty`. Not wired into `Military.can_enter` yet (step 2). Russian mobilization is left to step 8. Tests: `tests/diplomacy_test.gd`. Original plan: The nations (Russia, Serbia, Montenegro, Albania, Romania, Bulgaria, Greece, Ottoman Empire) already exist from step 0; minor Balkan states may stay passive until phase 6. Treaty targets to seed: Serbia-Russia, Austria's war on Serbia already under way at the start, Russian mobilization, Romania/Bulgaria/Greece/Ottoman neutral at start with hooks for later joins. Add `data/diplomacy.json`, `Treaty`, `Diplomacy` class, the `relations` table, and a loader for `blocs`, `neutral_pacts`, `guarantees` plus new `alliances` and `passage` entries in the scenario (Entente: FRA-RUS, GBR-FRA entente, Triple Alliance GER-AUH-ITA with ITA's defensive-only clause, Belgium guaranteed by GBR, GER, FRA). Remove ITA from the Central Powers bloc: it starts neutral.
   2. **Declarations and casus belli.** *(done)* `DeclareWar` (a, b, casus belli, optional war group) goes through `Diplomacy.declare_war`; `casus_belli_options(a, b)` lists what a nation can use today: `reconquest` and `border_claim` from the scenario's `claims` (province names; a border claim needs the province to touch the claimant), `guarantee_defence` (the target is at war with a nation you guarantee), `ally_called` (an ally is at war with the target), `unprovoked_aggression` (earned, dated) and always `none`. `declare_block` gives the reason a declaration is impossible: same, unknown, no_land, at_war, allied (an entente counts) or nap. A NAP is torn up first with `CancelTreaty` (costs the partner's opinion, everyone's opinion and prestige; costs per treaty kind in `data/diplomacy.json`, `war.cancel`). Each casus belli sets the target's opinion drop, the world's and prestige (`war.casus_belli`); `none` costs more and gives the aggressor's land neighbours and the victim's guarantors an `unprovoked_aggression` casus belli for five years plus 40 grievance (`GameState.casus_belli`, `GameState.grievance`; grievance cools 2 a month). A provoked AI nation (grievance 60+, with the casus belli) declares on the aggressor in the monthly step; the player never does it by rule. `War` records `casus_belli` and `war_group`; `Nation.prestige` is new. `Military.can_enter` now lets allies and passage-treaty holders cross (owner and holder must both allow it; ententes do not open land). Not done: join decisions for allies (step 3), the mobilization gate on unjustified wars (step 4), border-crossing and mobilization provocation (steps 3-4), a UI (step 7); the Military panel still uses `SetWar`. Tests: `tests/diplomacy_test.gd`. Original plan: `DeclareWar` command replaces `SetWar` as the way wars start (`SetWar` stays as a test/debug command). Needs a casus belli (`border_claim`, `guarantee_defence`, `ally_called`, `reconquest`, or `none` with a relations and war support penalty). Validity rules: not allied, no active NAP (breaking one costs relations with everyone and prestige), not already at war, and neutral borders stay closed until the war exists. Declaration runs the join logic below. Add `Military.can_enter` support for allies (passage through allied ground) and `passage` treaties.
   3. **Alliances and joining.** *(done)* Every declaration opens a conflict record (`GameState.war_groups`: original attacker and defender, members by side). `Diplomacy.offer_joins` asks every ally and guarantor of the members; each nation that joins opens pairwise wars (cb `ally_called` or `guarantee_defence`, same `war_group`) against every member of the other side and then brings its own allies in. Obligations: a guarantee binds when the guaranteed nation is the original target; an alliance binds as `alliance_defence` when the ally is the original target (defensive-only ones too) and as `alliance_called` otherwise unless it is defensive-only; an entente is optional (the AI joins if its opinion of an enemy is at or below `join.entente_join_relation`). Declining a bound obligation costs the ally's goodwill and prestige and, for defence and guarantees, ends the treaty (`join.decline` in `data/diplomacy.json`). A nation that would have to fight its own ally or pact partner declines for free. The AI always answers at once; the player gets a pending decision (`GameState.pending_joins`, signal `join_requested`) carrying the enemies and the price of each answer, `Clock` refuses to run while one waits, and `AnswerJoin` settles it. `Diplomacy.violate_border(violator, victim)` asks a guaranteed nation's guarantors without a war between the two; nothing calls it yet because armies cannot enter neutral ground. The starting Austro-Serbian war is registered as a conflict but calls no one: `offer_joins` is for step 4's crisis loop. Anglo-Portuguese alliance is defensive-only. Tests: `tests/diplomacy_test.gd`. Original plan: On a declaration, every ally of the target is offered a join decision (the player gets a prompt; the AI decides by rules): defensive alliances join automatically if the target was attacked, offensive ones only if called by the leader. Guarantors of the target get the join decision too (the Belgium case). Joining opens pairwise wars against the aggressor's side with `casus_belli = ally_called`/`guarantee_defence` and the same `war_group`. Declining breaks the obligation (relations penalty with the ally; a broken guarantee ends the treaty and costs prestige). Violation of a guaranteed nation's borders without a war declaration (an army entering Belgian ground) triggers the same decision.
   4. **Mobilization and the crisis loop.** *(done)* `Diplomacy.step_day` runs daily (constants in `data/diplomacy.json`, `crisis`). A nation at 20% mobilization alarms each land neighbour it is neither allied with nor at war with, once (`GameState.alarms`): their opinion of it drops 10, they resent it (+15 grievance) and the neighbour's allies are called up (`GameState.mobilization_calls`, entente partners excepted). AI nations answer: 60% of whoever alarms them, 80% of whoever alarmed an ally, 50% when called to a war, and a floor of 50% at war; they ease back 1% a day when nothing threatens them, and move up 5% a day. The player's own slider is never touched except by the war floor. Mobilization changes recompute the economy. AI nations bound to join a war no longer enter at once: they are *called* (`GameState.join_calls`, signal `join_called`), mobilize, and enter when they reach 40% (about eight days), then call their own allies. Player decisions are still immediate prompts. A war with no casus belli needs 30% mobilization first (`Diplomacy.declare_gate`), so aggression shows as an alarm before it happens. With the scenario's `start_crisis` the starting Austro-Serbian war calls Russia, Montenegro and Germany on day one and the general war follows from the rules alone (France is prompted when Russia enters). The default player's economy therefore starts to feel mobilization within days; set `start_crisis` to false in `scenario.json` for a quiet start. Tests: `tests/diplomacy_test.gd`. Original plan: Mobilization (already in Economy) becomes a visible diplomatic act: raising it above a threshold next to a neighbour lowers their relations and lets their allies call up too. Countries at war get war-time rules (mobilization floor, war support effects). A declaration can only happen after `mobilization >= cfg.min_mobilization` for aggressors with no casus belli, so a war cannot start from nothing and the 1914 chain can start, or not start, from systems alone.

6. **AI.** *(planned; detail below)* Goal scoring and war plans so the world plays itself. Agreed order: armies that move and fight on their own first, then the AI's diplomacy.

   **Starting point (what phase 5 left us).** Every nation but the player's already reacts through rules: mobilization follows alarms, nations bound to a war are called up and join, a data-driven plan (`war_plans`) makes Germany declare on Belgium, a provoked neighbour retaliates, weary or losing nations make peace overtures, and war support can force a white peace. What nobody does is *play*: AI armies never move, so the 1914 chain produces a general war with no battles, and it dies of stalemate and debt. The pieces the AI will drive already exist and are tested: `MoveArmy`, `RaiseUnits`, `DesignateFront` and `AssignToFront` (with `Military.step_fronts` spreading and holding a line), `Military.find_path`, `supply_cost`, `border_provinces`, `Peace.surrender_terms` and `acceptable`, `Diplomacy.can_declare`, `treaty_acceptable`, `casus_belli_options`. Gaps: `GameState.issue` recomputes the whole economy after every command, which is too slow for dozens of AI orders a day; there is no per-nation temperament; no army can cross the sea until phase 7, so a war against an enemy across water cannot be fought on land yet; armies see everything (there is no fog).

   **Design decisions.**
   - The AI plays every nation except the player's, through the same commands the player uses, so command-log replay, saving and tests stay honest. AI orders are logged but applied with a new `GameState.issue_quiet` that skips the economy recompute (the day's step does it once).
   - No scripts: behaviour is utility scoring over candidate actions, with weights in `data/ai.json` and per-nation traits and war plans as scenario data. A plan says what a nation wants (objectives with weights), never what it does turn by turn.
   - It is deterministic: no random numbers, ties broken by province id and tag, so a run can be replayed and a bug reproduced.
   - It does not cheat: it sees what the rules show everyone (no fog exists yet, so it sees every army), obeys supply, movement and the closed-borders rule, and pays for recruits and diplomacy like the player (diplomacy points included).
   - It is staggered: each nation thinks every `ai.think_days` days at its own offset, so the cost spreads over the month. Budget: well under a millisecond a day on average.
   - Defence uses the existing front system (it is already tested, drawn on the map and holds lines); attack uses direct orders. The player sees the AI's fronts on the map as they see their own.
   - The same rules and the same temperament apply to every nation. Temperament is two numbers, `aggression` and `caution` (0 to 1), with one default in `data/ai.json`: they scale the odds an AI wants before attacking, how much of its army it commits and how readily it digs in or pulls back. A scenario may give a nation its own values, but none does for now: depth and variety between nations come later.
   - No special cases. A nation whose enemy cannot be reached by land (the sea is in the way) simply has no attack targets, and defends: that falls out of the same path and `can_enter` checks every nation uses, until phase 7 adds transport.

   **Design decisions.**
   - The navy is a sibling of the army, not a rewrite of it. A `Fleet` (`scripts/sim/fleet.gd`) is a stack of `Ship`s standing in a **sea zone** or a **port**; it moves along a path of zones the way an army moves along provinces, and is driven by the same command pattern (`GameState.issue`, `issue_quiet` for the AI), so replay, tests and the AI work unchanged.
   - Sea zones are a second layer, not edits to the land bitmap. About 45 zones from the Atlantic to the Black Sea (North Sea, Skagerrak, Baltic in three, English Channel, Western Approaches, Bay of Biscay, Irish Sea, Gibraltar, the Mediterranean in three, Adriatic, Aegean, Black Sea, and so on). `tools/build_sea_zones.py` partitions the sea pixels from hand-placed seed points in `data/map/sea_zones.tsv` (nearest seed by water distance, so a zone never leaps a peninsula) and writes `data/map/sea_zones.png` (flat colours, like `provinces.png`), `sea_adjacency.tsv`, and `ports.tsv` (coastal province to the zone it opens on, with a second zone where it touches two). The tool validates: every sea pixel is in a zone, the zone graph is connected, every coastal province has a port zone. Zone ids are a separate id space (`Fleet.zone`, `MapData.sea_zones`), never mixed with province ids.
   - Scale is operational, like the land game: a ship counts as a **squadron** (a handful of hulls) and a fleet is a few dozen of them. Types in `data/navy.json`: dreadnought squadron, cruiser, destroyer flotilla, submarine flotilla, transport convoy. Each has strength, attack, speed, range, detection, upkeep, cost, build days and the port level it needs. Nothing is hard-coded.
   - Ports gate everything. Ships are built, repaired and resupplied only in a port of their nation (the existing `port` building; its level sets the largest type it can build and the repair rate). A fleet beyond `range` days from a friendly port loses supply and strength.
   - Control of the sea is a number per zone and nation, recomputed daily from the ships present, not a flag fought over. Blockade is derived from it, never set by hand: `Nation.blockade` becomes an output of the navy (`Navy.step_day` writes it before `Economy` reads it).
   - Naval upkeep is real money. `naval_budget` becomes the starting size of the fleet (a scenario table `navy.tsv`, calibrated so each nation's upkeep matches today's figure) and then a cost per ship. Ships compete with armies and buildings for the same treasury.
   - The AI plays the navy by the same rules as everything else: utility scoring, data weights in `data/ai.json`, deterministic, no cheating, one temperament for every nation, no scripts. Real 1914 behaviour (the High Seas Fleet staying in port, the Grand Fleet closing the North Sea) must come out of the weights and the odds, not special cases.
   - Polish is built last and on its own: nothing in steps 0 to 7 waits for it, and nothing in steps 8 to 12 changes the simulation's rules.

8. **Economic rework.** *(built; steps 0 to 8 below)* The economy is passive today: GDP comes from population density and a few percentage bonuses, buildings are "+X%" with money as their only limit, there are three resources, units cost money and recruits and nothing else, and the player's choices are one tax slider and where to build. The rework follows the Hearts of Iron IV economy (factories as capacity, production lines, equipment that units need, consumer goods, economy laws) simplified, with money kept in the loop.

   **Decisions so far.** Discrete factories (not smooth industry points); money plus materials per item made (not factory time alone, not money alone); a few equipment categories that units need; the economy law is separate from the mobilization slider; a minimal rule-based AI planner in the first version; ammunition is not its own category but part of a broad **Arms** resource. First version: factories, construction and consumer goods, plus equipment production lines. Out of scope for it: more resources, a trade market with prices, trade agreements, research, aircraft, a full AI rework, changes to the navy's own build system.

   **Design sketch.**
   - **Slots and factories.** Each province has building slots from population and infrastructure. Civilian factory, military factory and shipyard capacity use slots. Farmland, railways, ports, trading houses, mines, sawmills and the foreign ministry stay as they are and use none. Starting factory counts are calibrated from each nation's present industry, so 1914 revenue does not move.
   - **Money against guns.** GDP, and so tax, is partly driven by civilian factories. A military factory makes equipment instead of income: that is the tradeoff.
   - **Construction.** Civilian factories supply construction points to a national pool shared by a priority queue; each project can absorb only so many points a day, so nothing finishes at once. Buildings still cost money and materials.
   - **Consumer goods and economy laws.** A share of civilian industry feeds the population (about 35% in peace). Laws (peacetime, partial mobilization, war economy, total war) lower that share and lift military output at a cost in GDP and war support, and the higher ones need a war or high mobilization. Separate from the manpower mobilization slider.
   - **Equipment (proposed categories: Arms, Artillery, Transport).** *Arms* is small arms and ammunition together: infantry and cavalry need it, and fighting consumes it as well as casualties destroying it. *Artillery* is guns. *Transport* is wagons, trucks and rolling stock and sets how far supply reaches. Units draw their equipment from a national stockpile when raised; coverage below 100% weakens an army and slows its reinforcement. Ships stay with the Navy system.
   - **Production lines.** A line has a category, assigned military factories and an efficiency that grows the longer it runs and resets if the product changes. Each item costs money and materials as it is made; a shortage of either slows the line in proportion.
   - **AI (minimal).** Data weights in `data/ai.json`: assign factories to lines by equipment deficit, build factories by utility, choose a law by war state, keep to the same rules as the player.

   **Done so far (not committed).**

At the start Germany holds 98 civilian and 13 military factories and Britain 111 and 4, each province up to 40% more slots than it has factories (at least two); output is exactly what it was before (a factory counts only beyond or short of the calibrated `province_civ_base`). `factories` in `data/economy.json` holds the constants.

What is left: civilian factory, military factory, railway, port.

A project (`Economy.new_job`) needs `build_days` times its `rate` in points and takes at most its rate a day, so with ample capacity it still takes `build_days`, and with little it takes longer; the queue (`Nation.construction`) is served in order, the points left over are lost, and `PrioritizeConstruction` moves a project to the front.    - *Step 3, consumer goods and economy laws:* four laws in `data/economy.json` under `laws`: civilian economy (35% of the civilian factories' work on consumer goods), early mobilization (30%, military factories +10%, output -2%), war economy (20%, +25%, -6%, 0.3 war support lost a month at war) and total mobilization (10%, +40%, -12%, 0.8). The consumer share is what the construction pool gives up; military output will multiply the production lines of step 5; output multiplies GDP in `Economy.nation_budget`; weariness is part of `WarSupport.step_month`. `SetEconomyLaw` changes it: early mobilization needs a war or 20% mobilization, war economy a war, total mobilization a war and 50% mobilization; a higher law than the one in force waits 30 days from the last change, a lower one never waits; each month a law whose requirements stopped holding is dropped a step (`Economy.enforce_laws`). `Nation.economy_law` and `law_changed_day` are saved with the rest. The Economy window's overview has the selector (laws not open are greyed, with the reason) and a note on what the law does. The AI does not change law yet (step 7). `tests/economy_laws_test.gd`.

   - *Step 4, equipment and stockpiles:* two categories in `data/military.json` under `equipment`, Arms (thousands of rifles) and Artillery (batteries); each unit type lists `needs` for a full-strength unit. Equipment is held by the units (`Unit.equip`), not by a national pool: coverage is held over need at the unit's present strength, and a missing share costs power down to a per-category floor (35% for Arms, 50% for Artillery). Casualties and attrition destroy equipment with the men; a unit in a fight uses 1% of its full Arms need a day. Each nation also has a store (`Nation.equipment`); a supplied unit that is not fighting draws what it lacks from it, up to 5% of its full need a day, and a trained unit is issued whatever the store holds when training ends. Until the production lines of step 5, each military factory (plus one for arsenals and imports) adds to the store daily, times the economy law's military figure, and the store at the start is 120 days of that; with these figures the first peace in the AI history run still comes on day 157. Transport waits (decision a); construction speed is capped nationally only (c). `tests/equipment_test.gd`. No interface for it yet (step 8).

   - *Step 5, production lines:* `Production` (scripts/sim/production.gd) replaces the stopgap output. A nation's military factories, plus one for arsenals and imports, are assigned to lines in `Nation.production_lines`; a line makes one category (output a day = factories x the category's `output` x efficiency x the economy law's military figure). Efficiency runs from 40% to 100% over 90 days of running and starts over if the line changes product. Each item costs money (taken from the treasury each day, counted in `Nation.equipment_spent`) and iron or coal (from the stockpile); a line short of either makes only what it can pay for and says which. While `Nation.lines_auto` is set, which it is for everyone at the start and for the AI until step 7, the factories are shared half and half between Arms and Artillery as their number changes, which gives the same output as before. `SetProductionLine` opens, changes and closes lines (taking the player off automatic) and `SetProductionAuto` puts it back. The Economy window has a Production tab: the store, free factories, the Automatic switch, and a row per line with its product, factory buttons, efficiency, monthly output and cost. `tests/production_test.gd`, `tests/production_ui_test.gd`. The budget's net does not yet count this spending, about 15 million a year for Germany.

   - *Step 6, military integration:* raising a unit takes the equipment it needs from the national store when the order is given and sets it aside in the training job (`RaiseUnits`; what the store cannot give the unit goes without, and anything the lines make during training tops it up when it arrives). Missing equipment already cut a unit's power (step 4); now it also slows reinforcement, to between `reinforce_floor` (50%) and the full rate by the unit's worst coverage. Arms in combat are used up as in step 4. Transport and supply reach are untouched (decision a). The army list marks an army below 95% equipped (orange below 70%), the army detail gives coverage per category, and the Recruit tab shows what the selected units need against the store, in orange when it falls short. Tests added to `tests/equipment_test.gd` and `tests/military_ui_test.gd`.

   - *Step 7, minimal AI:* `AIProduction` (scripts/ai/ai_production.gd), once a month after the budget, with weights in `data/ai.json` under `production`; it uses the player's commands. *Lines:* `Production.deficit` works out the share of its equipment need a nation lacks (units' needs less what they hold, what is set aside for units in training and the store); the military factories are shared by the automatic split bent 60% toward the category lacked most, and while nothing is lacking the AI stays on automatic production, so 1914 is unchanged. Only the size of each line changes, never its product, so efficiency is kept. *Law:* civilian in peace; early mobilization at any war or from 20% mobilization; war economy at war when the worst deficit reaches 25%; total mobilization at 60% with war support at least 40; up as far as the requirements and the wait allow, straight down in peace. *Factories:* `AIBudget.build_return` counts a military factory as worth 20 times the worst deficit a year (1.5 times at war), so the AI builds them only when equipment is short. The AI history markers hold (first peace on day 157). `tests/ai_production_test.gd`. Also fixed: a front whose army can find no route to its slot (a peacetime front across a closed border) searched for one every day and slowed the day to 300 ms; it now retries every ten days (`Front.retry`).

   - *Step 8, interface, tutorial and tuning:* the budget now counts the lines' spending (`Production.planned_spend`, the Equipment row of the Economy window's spending, `budget.equipment`); the treasury pays it a day at a time and the month adds it back so it is not taken twice. The first `free_factories` (2) factories' worth of money is not charged, because the arsenals are kept in army upkeep; this keeps every nation's 1914 budget balanced as before. Materials the stockpile lacks are bought at the import markup, not past a blockade (a line cannot live off a stockpile that imports never refill; Germany's iron had run dry). The nation window shows equipment in store, military factories and the economy law; the tutorial has an Equip your army step (13 in all). Money costs per item were set low (Arms 0.004, Artillery 0.01 GBP millions). *Tuning:* a day's cost over 400 days of AI play is 47 ms against 44 ms before the rework (44 and 98 on the last commit, which had the front-search slowdown); over two years the major powers keep making equipment, stay fully equipped, no treasury runs away (worst -2) and no law outlives its war. `tests/economy_run_test.gd`.

15. **Economic rework 2.** *(in progress, from the owner's specification)* The economy becomes a daily system of flows and industrial capacity (IC), replacing the monthly, money-priced resource balance and the money cost of buildings and equipment. Money stays for taxes, upkeep, debt and diplomacy.

   **Decisions by the owner.** (1) Buildings and equipment cost IC and materials only, no money. (2) Trade is automatic: a nation short of a resource imports it, locking civilian factories at the trade rate; blockades cut it. (3) The naval dockyard exists as a building but ships keep the current build system until a navy pass. (4) Railways keep today's hop-based supply reach; levels extend it and burn coal, and with no coal they stop helping.

   **Defaults taken (change them in the data files).** A "state" of the specification is a province. "National Unity" is war support. Per-hour effects are per day of combat. Resource figures are units a day: one unit a day is 1,000 kt a year of the old figures (Germany: coal 225, iron 28, timber 25 a day). Chemicals are made by industry (a nation figure times its industrial output, `chemicals` in `data/nations.json`) and can be stockpiled; draft animals come from farmland; nitrates come from Haber-Bosch plants and from an overseas supply that blockades cut. An IC "item" cost is calibrated so that a military factory at the 50% efficiency cap makes what it made at 100% before.

   **Resources** (`data/economy.json`, `resources`): coal (stockpiles, 1 civilian factory buys 8 a day, shortage costs up to 100% of what it powers), iron or steel (flow only, 8, 100%), nitrates (flow, 4, 90%), draft animals (flow, 10, 50%), chemicals (stockpiles, 4, 100%), timber (flow, 12, 75%). Flow resources cannot be saved: a surplus is lost (or exported), a shortage hits at once. Coal and chemicals keep a stockpile that covers shortages.

   **Buildings.** Civilian factory (10,800 IC; 5.0 IC of construction a day; at most 20 a province; pays the consumer share and trades for resources), military factory (7,200 IC; 4.5 IC of equipment a day; 20), naval dockyard (6,400 IC; 2.5 IC a day; 10; coastal; still hidden: yard capacity comes from the port level), Haber-Bosch plant (15,000 IC; 2 nitrates a day; 3). Province works: trench complex (1,200 IC a level, ten levels, +15% defense and 5% less shell damage a level), heavy fortification (8,000 IC for the first level, rising by half again each level, ten levels, +30% defense and 10% less hard attack a level), railway (2,500 IC a level, five levels, burns 0.5 coal a day a level). A project takes in at most its cost over a minimum number of days a day, so construction is capped by capacity and by the project.

   **Production.** Shells are a third equipment category (1.5 IC, 1 iron and 2 nitrates a unit) with a national stockpile; artillery in combat burns them daily, and with an empty stockpile every artillery unit loses 75% of its attack. A line starts at 15% efficiency and rises to the 50% cap; changing to a newer version of the same equipment keeps 85% of the efficiency, changing to a different kind keeps 20%.

   **Macro pressures.** Labor: factory output is multiplied by 1 - 1.2 times the share of the people under arms. Consumer goods: civilian factories locked = all of them times the economy law's share plus war weariness (1% for every 100,000 casualties, easing in peace). Blockade: import throughput is the convoys that get through less the enemy's naval supremacy; below 30% the nation starves: -30% factory output and 0.1 war support lost a day.

   **Daily order** (`Economy.step_day`, then the military, then the rest): labor, domestic resources, blockades, trade and convoys, consumer goods, coal consumption, ammunition (in combat), production lines, construction, efficiency and stability.

   **Progress.** Built, with tests: 1 resources and automatic trade; 2 buildings and construction in IC; 3 the daily pressures (labor 1.2 times the share under arms, weariness 1% of civilian factories per 100,000 men lost, starvation under 30% of imports: -30% output and -0.1 war support a day, `tests/pressures_test.gd`); 4 shells and the production lines (IC item costs and no money, efficiency 15% to 50% in 120 days, 20% kept when the kind changes, shells burned by artillery in battle with -75% attack when none are left); 5 trenches and fortifications (defense for the owner's army in its province, shelling cut by trenches and assault by fortifications, `tests/works_test.gd`) and railway levels (cheaper supply hops, coal-fired: with a coal shortage they stop helping movement and supply). 6 is in progress: the Economy overview, Construction header and tutorial speak IC; the AI digs trenches and forts behind its fronts at war (`front_works_bonus`). Not done: retuning the AI speed limits, rebalancing trade for landlocked and tiny nations (Switzerland, Serbia, Montenegro and Albania start short of iron or timber), a fully realistic shell consumption (`shells` a day in `unit_types.artillery`, 0.15 for now), naval dockyards and ships on IC. Britain, Serbia and Montenegro had `development` raised so 1914 budgets balance without export income.

   **Build order.**
   0. **Calibration and data.** Factory counts and slots per province, starting stockpiles, equipment per unit type, all in data files; a calibration report so 1914 revenue and army sizes match today's.
   1. **Factories and slots.** Civilian and military factory buildings, slot rules, GDP split, `Economy` refactor. Tests: starting revenue unchanged within a tolerance; converting a factory moves income into capacity.
   2. **Construction capacity.** The point pool, queue priorities, parallel builds, per-project rate cap; the queue panel shows points and ETA.
   3. **Consumer goods and economy laws.** The consumer share, the four laws, a command to change law, gating, effects on tax, output and war support.
   4. **Equipment and stockpiles.** Categories, unit needs, coverage, losses in battle, saving and loading of stocks.
   5. **Production lines.** The line model, efficiency, money and materials cost, a Production panel.
   6. **Military integration.** Training draws equipment, coverage weakens armies, Arms are consumed in combat, Transport sets supply reach.
   7. **Minimal AI.** The planner above; the 1914 AI history markers must still hold (the phase 6 and 7 history tests).
   8. **Interface, tutorial and tuning.** The Production panel, Economy panel changes, tutorial steps, a two-year AI run, and a day step no more than about a quarter dearer than today's.

   **Risks.** Every phase 6 and 7 test and the AI history markers rest on today's numbers and will need recalibrating; units needing equipment changes recruitment, the AI's budget and the war's pace; the day step must stay cheap with production lines for every nation.

   **Open decisions.** Settled: (a) Transport waits, (b) equipment is held by the units with a national store behind them, (c) construction is capped nationally only.

(3) The naval dockyard exists as a building but ships keep the current build system until a navy pass. (4) Railways keep today's hop-based supply reach; levels extend it and burn coal, and with no coal they stop helping.

   **Defaults taken (change them in the data files).** A "state" of the specification is a province. "National Unity" is war support. Per-hour effects are per day of combat. Resource figures are units a day: one unit a day is 1,000 kt a year of the old figures (Germany: coal 225, iron 28, timber 25 a day). Chemicals are made by industry (a nation figure times its industrial output, `chemicals` in `data/nations.json`) and can be stockpiled; draft animals come from farmland; nitrates come from Haber-Bosch plants and from an overseas supply that blockades cut. An IC "item" cost is calibrated so that a military factory at the 50% efficiency cap makes what it made at 100% before.

   **Resources** (`data/economy.json`, `resources`): coal (stockpiles, 1 civilian factory buys 8 a day, shortage costs up to 100% of what it powers), iron or steel (flow only, 8, 100%), nitrates (flow, 4, 90%), draft animals (flow, 10, 50%), chemicals (stockpiles, 4, 100%), timber (flow, 12, 75%). Flow resources cannot be saved: a surplus is lost (or exported), a shortage hits at once. Coal and chemicals keep a stockpile that covers shortages.

   **Buildings.** Civilian factory (10,800 IC; 5.0 IC of construction a day; at most 20 a province; pays the consumer share and trades for resources), military factory (7,200 IC; 4.5 IC of equipment a day; 20), naval dockyard (6,400 IC; 2.5 IC a day; 10; coastal; still hidden: yard capacity comes from the port level), Haber-Bosch plant (15,000 IC; 2 nitrates a day; 3). Province works: trench complex (1,200 IC a level, ten levels, +15% defense and 5% less shell damage a level), heavy fortification (8,000 IC for the first level, rising by half again each level, ten levels, +30% defense and 10% less hard attack a level), railway (2,500 IC a level, five levels, burns 0.5 coal a day a level). A project takes in at most its cost over a minimum number of days a day, so construction is capped by capacity and by the project.

   **Production.** Shells are a third equipment category (1.5 IC, 1 iron and 2 nitrates a unit) with a national stockpile; artillery in combat burns them daily, and with an empty stockpile every artillery unit loses 75% of its attack. A line starts at 15% efficiency and rises to the 50% cap; changing to a newer version of the same equipment keeps 85% of the efficiency, changing to a different kind keeps 20%.

   **Macro pressures.** Labor: factory output is multiplied by 1 - 1.2 times the share of the people under arms. Consumer goods: civilian factories locked = all of them times the economy law's share plus war weariness (1% for every 100,000 casualties, easing in peace). Blockade: import throughput is the convoys that get through less the enemy's naval supremacy; below 30% the nation starves: -30% factory output and 0.1 war support lost a day.

   **Daily order** (`Economy.step_day`, then the military, then the rest): labor, domestic resources, blockades, trade and convoys, consumer goods, coal consumption, ammunition (in combat), production lines, construction, efficiency and stability.

   **Progress.** Steps 1 and 2 are built and their tests updated: the economy, construction, factories, production, map mode and visibility tests follow the IC model (the economy window's tutorial text still needs a pass). Landlocked and tiny nations can start short of iron or timber because trade needs civilian factories to lock; Britain, Serbia and Montenegro had `development` raised so 1914 budgets balance without export income. Steps 3 to 6 are not started.

   **Build order.** 1. Resources, daily flows and automatic trade. 2. Buildings and construction in IC. 3. The daily engine, labor, weariness and the blockade. 4. Shells and production lines. 5. Trench, fortification, railway coal and draft animals in the military. 6. Interface, AI and tuning (the AI history markers and the speed limits will be retuned). Each step keeps the tests passing.

**Major cities.** `data/scenarios/july_1914/cities.tsv` lists 98 cities (name, province, map pixel, rough 1910-1914 population, rank 1 capital / 2 over 500,000 / 3 other, and whether the population is an estimate), built by `tools/build_cities.py` from Natural Earth's populated places (modern names and positions) with a hand-written table of 1914 names and populations for the cities that mattered; the rest are a share of today's population. `GameState.cities` and `cities_by_province` hold them (static data, not saved). `CityLayer` (scripts/map/city_layer.gd) draws a marker and name at a constant screen size: capitals always, great cities from zoom 0.3, large ones from 0.65, a name that would land on one already written is dropped, and nothing in the Construction mode. The province card names the city and its population. Not yet a game element: a city gives nothing in play. `tests/cities_test.gd`.

**3D map (a mode).** `Map3D` (scripts/map/map_3d.gd), switched with V or the Settings window, off by default. The 2D map and every layer on it are moved into an off-screen `SubViewport` (1.5 times the window) and its picture is laid on a flat plane that a perspective `Camera3D` looks at; looking straight down (90 degrees) it is the flat map, and Page Up, Page Down, Shift and the wheel, or Ctrl and the middle button tilt it (35 to 90). Panning and zooming still move the 2D camera inside the picture. The mouse is followed through the plane (a camera ray meets it, the hit becomes a pixel of the picture) and the click is pushed into the viewport, so selecting, orders, box-select and hover work as before; the windows and the top bar stay on screen. Switching off puts the layers back in their order. Not done: yaw (turning the map), real relief or 3D unit models. `tests/map_3d_test.gd`; `tools/screenshot_3d.gd` takes pictures and checks clicks through the plane.

**Look of the interface.** `UiTheme` (scripts/ui/ui_theme.gd) gives every control the same look: slate panels, gold rules and corner studs (a nine-patch frame drawn in code, `UiTheme.ornate`, used by windows, the main menu and the side panels), cream serif text (a `SystemFont` that tries Georgia, Palatino, Cambria and Times New Roman), brown buttons with a gold rim, gold tabs and slider grooves, a gold top bar and parchment-coloured buttons on the map mode palette (`UiTheme.parchment`). `UiTheme.frame` and `UiTheme.compact` serve the blocks along the bottom. The mouse pointer is drawn the same way (`UiCursor`, scripts/ui/ui_cursor.gd): a cream arrow in a dark outline with a gold rule, and a gold pointing hand over clickable controls, installed by `UiSettings.apply_saved`. No image files are used. `tools/screenshot_panel.gd` and `tools/screenshot_menu.gd` save pictures of the real window.

**Ships on IC (M3.4).** A squadron costs IC (`ic` in `data/navy.json`: battle squadron 10,000 over at least 720 days, cruiser 4,000/420, destroyer 1,500/200, submarine 1,200/240, transport 1,000/150) drawn each day from the nation's construction pool, hulls first and buildings after (`Economy.step_day`), at most `ic / build_days` a day each; materials still come from the stockpile with any shortfall bought at the import markup, and that purchase is the only money a ship costs besides upkeep. Port level still sets the slots and the minimum port. `RaiseShips` queues jobs with `points_total`, `points_left`, `rate`, `given`; `Navy._build` launches a hull when its points are in. The AI lays down a hull only if the hulls on the slips draw at most `build.income_share` of the pool (`data/ai.json`). Tests: `tests/navy_test.gd`.

**War justification (M4.1).** `JustifyWar` (25 points, `points.costs.justify`) starts a 90-day effort (`war.justify` in `data/diplomacy.json`, `GameState.justifications`, at most two at a time) to manufacture a *fabricated claim* casus belli (target relations -35, world -5, prestige -2; declaring on it costs 10 points). The target resents it (grievance +8); the effort is dropped if the two go to war, ally or sign a pact; the finished claim lasts `cb_years` and raises `casus_belli_ready`. A war with no justification still exists and costs 30 points, prestige -15, relations -50/-20 and war support -10. The AI (`AIDiplomacy.consider_justify`) starts an effort against a neighbour when it has a plan, the temper and the odds but no casus belli, rather than striking unjustified. The Diplomacy window has a Justify war button with the days left. Tests: `tests/justify_test.gd`.

**Gifts and prestige uses (M4.2, M4.3).** A gift costs 10 points and raises the receiver's opinion by `gift_max_relation (20) * (1 - e^(-N/ref)) * e^(-recent/ref)`, `ref` being 5% of the receiver's yearly revenue (at least £2M): small nations are swayed more, big gifts give diminishing returns, and the sender's recent gifts to the same nation (`GameState.gift_memory`, fading 10% a month) weigh against the next. Prestige now buys things besides point income (`points.prestige_uses`): each point cuts a justification's price by 1% (up to 40%, and a bad name raises it) and adds half a point of regard to its treaty offers (up to 15), so a high-prestige nation gets alliances others would refuse. Tests: `tests/gift_test.gd`.

**Named alliances (M4.4).** An alliance signed in play (`Diplomacy.propose_treaty`, by the player or the AI) is named after its two members ("Switzerland–Netherlands Alliance", stored in the treaty's `terms.name`, saved with it); a nation that signs with a member of a named group joins it under the same name. `Diplomacy.bloc_name` gives the name to show: the scenario's historic bloc (Entente, Central Powers; `is_historic_bloc`), else the made alliance, else none. `RenameAlliance` lets a member give the group its own name (not available for historic blocs). The country panel and Diplomacy window show the name (the latter marks historic ones), the Diplomacy window has a rename field for an ally, and the Rankings chart shows each nation's bloc beside its name (tooltips in the table). Blocs still decide AI acceptance and the map outlines; only the scenario blocs do. Tests: `tests/alliance_names_test.gd`.

**Norway and Sweden (M5.1).** Both nations are on the map and in the scenario (22 nations). `tools/extend_map.py scandinavia` painted 57 provinces (21 Norwegian counties, 36 Swedish ones, the larger counties split in two to five) over the old grey background, using Natural Earth admin-1 data; the frame still ends at 67 N, so Finnmark, Troms and the north of Norrbotten are cut off. The adjacency, coastal, border and sea-zone builders were re-run (the Sound links Copenhagen and Skane as a strait); `tools/add_scandinavia.py` adds population (Norway 2.36 million, Sweden 5.45 million, urban shares by Natural Earth city populations), resources (Swedish iron of 7,000 kt a year, mostly Kiruna and Malmberget, which lie just north of the frame, is put on the northernmost Norrbotten piece; Bergslagen has the rest; Skane has the 400 kt of coal) and terrain. Scenario entries: development 1.29 (Norway) and 1.21 (Sweden) give revenues of 8.0 and 16.5 million, agri_yield 0.09 and 0.19 give food balances of 0.61 and 0.96 (Norway's farmland is tiny), intensities match 1913 coal, iron and timber use; armies of 20,000 and 65,000 in 3 and 4 commands; one small fleet each (Karlskrona, Horten); Britain, France, Germany and Russia guarantee Norway (1907 Integrity Treaty); Norway and Sweden like each other, Sweden fears Russia and leans toward Germany. Flags drawn as SVG (Nordic crosses). Tests: `tests/scandinavia_test.gd`. History checks adjusted: the German blockade builds more slowly because Swedish ore comes over the Baltic, and the great war now ends earlier (day 188), so the debt check samples when it ends.

**National focus (M5.2).** `Focus` (scripts/sim/focus.gd, data in `data/focus.json`): each nation works on one focus at a time (`Nation.focus` {id, days_left, days_total}, `focus_done`, `focus_fx`); `SetFocus` starts one (an empty id drops it, losing the progress), `Focus.step_day` runs it and `Focus.complete` applies the effects. A tree is the nation's template (`major`: eight foci in three rows, `minor`: four) plus its own extras: 10 foci for the seven great powers, 4 or 5 for the rest, 119 in all. Lasting effects are summed in `focus_fx` and read by `Economy.modifiers` (gdp, industry, food, trade, growth), `Economy.construction_points` (IC a day) and `Diplomacy.point_income_parts` (points a month); one-off effects are prestige, diplomacy points, treasury, war support, army size, relations and claims (a dated `national_claim` casus belli). `AIFocus` picks the best open focus for a nation with none in hand (the `ai` weight, plus need: war favours army and construction, peace favours output, hunger favours food; claims only for nations at least `min_aggression`), called from `AI.think`. The Focus window (`FocusPanel`, button or O) draws the tree with links, shows the one in hand with a progress bar, and the chosen focus's effects with Start and Drop. Tests: `tests/focus_test.gd`.

**The late-game AI (M5.3).** `AIAmbition` (scripts/ai/ai_ambition.gd, `ambition` in `data/ai.json`): from a year after the start (`start_delay_days`, so the 1914 opening plays as scripted), every `review_days` an AI nation at peace with an aggression of at least 0.4 scores its land neighbours (no truce, not allied, odds at least 1.2 times what it needs): a casus belli in hand, how little they like it, grievance, a smaller economy, and the margin of its odds, less a penalty per guarantor. The best above the threshold becomes `GameState.ai_ambition[tag]`: a dynamic offensive plan (objectives on the border provinces and the capital, flagged `dynamic`) joins `ai_plans`, `Diplomacy.mobilization_target` raises its mobilization to 0.4, `AIDiplomacy.consider_justify` works for a casus belli, and the usual war rules declare. It ends on a war, when the target stops being a fit, or after 540 days, and waits 270 days before the next. `AIForces.rebuild` raises troops at the capital in peace when a nation is under 85% of its peacetime army. Foci can be `repeatable` (up to `max` times, `Nation.focus_count`, effects adding up), so the AI always has something to work on; in a six-year run foci stay at 30 to 110 a year. `tools/late_report.gd` prints a year-by-year count of wars, peaces, treaties, claims and foci: after year one it shows 10 to 20 wars begun a year (including those joined by alliance) with a general war around years three and six. Cost: a four-year profile (`tools/profile_days.gd`) averages 86 ms a day (military 30, AI 21, diplomacy 11) with single military days of up to a second in the biggest wars; this is worth a look. Tests: `tests/ai_ambition_test.gd`.

**3D map, second stage (M6.1, M6.2).** The 3D map can be turned (`[` and `]`, 15 degrees; Ctrl and the middle button sideways; Home resets; `Map3D.yaw`), and the WASD keys follow the screen (`Camera2D.pan_rotation`). It has real terrain relief: `tools/build_relief.py` fetches open Terrarium elevation tiles (zoom 6, covering the map frame, kept in a cache outside the repository), resamples them into the map's frame at a quarter of the bitmap and writes `data/map/relief.png` (8-bit grey, the square root of the height, 3,000 m at 255). The plane is a 260 by 146 grid raised by a vertex shader from that picture, exaggerated 22 times and never more than a unit high, and lit from the north west by shading from the slope; the shader is told which part of the map the picture shows each frame (`_view_origin`, `_view_size` from the sub-viewport's canvas transform). A click is followed down onto the raised ground (`to_picture` repeats the ray against the surface five times with `height_units`, read from the picture on the CPU), so selecting and ordering work on the hills at any tilt or turn. The Settings window has a "Terrain relief in 3D" switch (`relief_on`). Tools: `tools/screenshot_relief.gd`. Tests: `tests/map_3d_test.gd`.

**Unit models in 3D (M6.3).** While the 3D map is on, `ArmyLayer` and `FleetLayer` stop drawing their banners (`markers_only`) and list them instead (`collect_markers`: province or place, nation, men, count, selected, fighting, estimate). `Map3D` stands a model on the ground for each (a pooled `Node3D`: a dark pole with a flag in the nation's colour on a black or yellow (selected) or red (fighting) backing, and a count in a `Label3D` for an army; a hull with a flag for a fleet; bigger armies stand taller, `standee_height`), refreshed every third frame and capped at 220 in view. A click or hover on a model is sent to the place it stands (`marker_at`), so models are picked as their banners were; the expanded army cards and every other layer are unchanged. The Settings window has a "Unit models in 3D" switch (`markers_on`); with it off the flat banners are drawn on the 3D map as before. Tests: `tests/map_3d_test.gd`.

**Land texture (after the 3D review).** The land is painted, not flat colour: `tools/build_landtex.py` fetches Terrarium elevation tiles (zoom 7), resamples them at half the map's size and paints every pixel from its height, slope, latitude and longitude as farmland, forest, steppe, scrub, desert, boreal forest, bare rock or snow (steppe east of 27 E and south of 52 N, the Spanish Meseta, the Anatolian plateau, North Africa and Scandinavia's taiga and tundra come from simple rules, with noise to break up the bands), lit from the north west with valleys darkened, into `data/map/land.jpg` (3928 by 2576, mipmapped on import). Satellite imagery was not an option: the only hosts reachable from the build machine are GitHub, the npm registry and the elevation bucket. `shaders/map.gdshader` blends it in under borders and labels (`land_texture`, `land_blend`, `land_natural`, `land_washed`): the nation's colour is lit by the ground's light and dark, and the ground's own colours come in over it (`MapView.land_natural_for`, up to 70% close up), so from afar the political colours read as before (20%) and zoomed in the terrain shows (90%). It applies in the Political, Diplomacy and Military modes, not under data maps; the 3D map asks for it as if zoomed in to 0.9 (`land_zoom_floor`) and draws its relief and shading more gently (exaggeration 16, shading 0.8) because the texture carries the hills too. Settings has a "Land texture" switch. Tests: `tests/land_texture_test.gd`; picture tool: `tools/screenshot_land.gd`.

**Satellite land texture.** The land has two textures to choose from (Settings, "Land texture": Satellite, Painted or Off, kept in `settings.cfg` as `map/land_style`; `MapView.land_style`, default Satellite). *Satellite* (`data/map/land_satellite.jpg`, `tools/build_landsat.py`) is cut from Natural Earth II with shaded relief, water and drainages (public domain; the 10 m raster `NE2_HR_LC_SR_W_DR` in the natural-earth-raster repository, 60 pixels to the degree), which is where the rivers and lakes come from. Its pale atlas tints are used only to say how green or dry the ground is: they pick a colour from a deeper, more photographic palette (dark forest, olive fields, steppe, sand), its shaded relief carries over as light and dark, grain of fields and woods is added, snow and bare rock are kept, and a fine hill detail from the elevation is added; the sea (and a hair of shore) takes the colour of the nearest land so no blue shows along the map's own coast. *Painted* (`data/map/land_painted.jpg`, `tools/build_landtex.py`) is the earlier version drawn from the elevation and rules of climate. The shader samples the texture without the `source_color` conversion (the canvas works in sRGB; with it the colours came out dull). The blend follows the zoom (`land_blend_for`: 30% far to 90% near; `land_natural_for`: 20% to 90% of the ground's own colour for Satellite, 10% to 70% for Painted); the 3D map asks for it as if zoomed to 0.65. Tests: `tests/land_texture_test.gd`.

**Cities in 3D.** Every city of the scenario (101 in `cities.tsv`) stands on the 3D map as a model of buildings (`CityModels`, scripts/map/city_models.gd; Settings "City models in 3D", `Map3D.cities_on`). The model is built in code from the city's number and its people (`CityModels.build`, a `SurfaceTool` mesh with vertex colours): the footprint grows with the square root of the population (0.1 to 0.5 plane units), and so do the number of buildings (28 for a town of 20,000, 140 or more for a great city), how tall the middle of town is, and the landmarks (a church spire from 15,000 people, a cathedral with a second tower from 250,000, a gilded dome for a capital); a province with factories adds chimneys (`Cities.chimneys`, one for every three, at most six). The same city always looks the same; a flag in the colour of whoever *controls* the city flies over it, so an occupied city shows the occupier. The people are *dynamic*: `Cities.population` is the scenario's figure times the ratio of the province's people now to what they were at the start (`GameState.province_pop_base`, read again on every load and so never saved; held between 0.4 and 3), and a model is rebuilt (at most four a frame) when the population moves into another size class (`bucket_of`, about 15% apart) or the chimneys change. Models appear by rank as the map is brought closer (capitals from afar, great cities from 0.25, others from 0.55), grow a little with the zoom, are lit by a sun from the north west (the 3D scene now has a directional light and ambient light), and replace the flat diamonds (`CityLayer.markers_hidden`; the names stay). A click on a model goes to the city's place on the map. Tests: `tests/city_models_test.gd`; pictures: `tools/screenshot_cities3d.gd`.

**Cities in 3D, second version.** The models are now laid out in map pixels, on the same scale as the ground (`CityModels.build(city_id, pop, chimneys, capital, centre, is_land)`): streets in a rotated grid, terraced houses with tiled gable roofs in blocks and taller flat-roofed blocks toward the middle (at most five storeys), an uneven edge, a church (towns of 12,000 and up) or a cathedral with two copper-roofed towers (250,000 and up), a palace with a gilded dome for a capital, and sheds and brick chimneys for a province with factories; the grey ground disc is gone. A building stands only where its whole foot is land (`CityModels.land_at` reads the province bitmap), and so does each landmark (`_land_spot` tries other places near the middle), so nothing hangs over a coast. The models scale with the map (`Map3D.units_per_px`) and fade in by rank as the camera comes closer instead of appearing at once (`opacity_at`, `FADE_ZOOM`: capitals from zoom 0.45 to 0.95, great cities 0.6 to 1.15, the rest 0.8 to 1.35): from afar the nations' colours show alone, and a city faint on its way in is tinted toward its holder's colour, so it rises out of the political colours.

## Cities in 3D, third version: landmarks and living cities

- `CityBuilder` (scripts/map/city_builder.gd) builds the mesh of a city; `CityParts` has the shape primitives (box, gable, cylinder, cone, frustum, dome, onion, spire, ring, strip, beam); `CityLandmarks` has about 40 famous buildings (Big Ben and Tower Bridge, the Eiffel Tower, the Brandenburg Gate, St Peter's and the Colosseum, the Kremlin, Hagia Sophia, Cologne Cathedral ...), placed by `data/landmarks.json` (city name -> type, position as fractions of the city radius, scale, rotation) in 85 cities. A city with landmarks gets no generic church; houses keep clear of their footprints and each is land-checked.
- What the province has shows in the model (`Cities.features`): a harbour with quay, warehouses, cranes and ships where there is a port (grey warships with a naval base), a railway station with tracks, a wall with towers and bastions where there are fortifications, and chimneys for factories. A city is rebuilt when its size class or its features change.
- Smoke: chimneys send up thin grey puffs; a city whose province is being taken (`GameState.occupation`) burns with thick black smoke from several fires. The puffs are one MultiMesh per city (billboards, risen in `CityModels._process`); CPUParticles3D drew huge black squares in the Compatibility renderer, so they are not used.

## Unit models, second version (3D)

`UnitModels` (scripts/map/unit_models.gd) builds the models of the 3D map's armies and fleets from `CityParts` primitives. Every unit (division) of an army is one model of its own, a soldier (rifle, pack, spiked helmet; the first carries the flag), a horseman or a field gun with a gunner, in the nation's colour blended to a uniform tone (Germany field-grey); the make-up (`comp`) comes from the army layer's markers and is shown as at most 9 units, each kind kept. A fleet is a line of up to 8 ships, each class (dreadnought, cruiser, destroyer, submarine, transport) with its own hull, bridge, funnels and turrets, the nation's colour as a deck stripe. Meshes are cached by what they show, so size classes, not every man, change the model. The models come up (`Map3D.model_growth`, zoom 0.9 to 1.6) in place of the pole and hull; the flag and count stay above them. `tests/unit_models_test.gd`; `tools/screenshot_units3d.gd`.
Not done: facing the way an army marches, walking, combat effects, a settings switch for the models only.
