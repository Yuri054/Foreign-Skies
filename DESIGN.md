# Grand Strategy Map: Design Plan

## Vision
Age of History 3's accessibility (flat 2D map, quick to learn, any nation playable) with Paradox-style depth in diplomacy, economy and war. The map is the interface: map modes carry most of the information.

## Locked decisions
| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Setting | Real world, stylized but semi-realistic map, realistic data |
| Time | Real time with pause, 5 speeds, day-based clock |
| First playable | Western Europe, WW1 scenario (start: July 1914). Basic military, political and economic systems. No scripted events. |
| Map | Hand-drawn province bitmap (EU4/CK3 style) |
| Multiplayer | None (single-player only) |

## Scenario: July 1914, Western Europe
**Playable/major:** United Kingdom, France, German Empire, Belgium, Netherlands, Luxembourg, Switzerland, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Denmark.
**Edge stubs (AI-driven, partly on-map):** Austria-Hungary, Russia. Their fronts are abstracted so the Western theatre is complete.
**Blocs:** Entente (UK, France, Russia) vs Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary). Italy starts neutral.
**Systemic (non-scripted) triggers:** alliance obligations, guarantees (UK guarantees Belgium), mobilization, and war declarations. The war should be able to start, or not start, from system rules alone.

## Core systems (v1)

### Political
- Government type and war support (0-100). War support drops with casualties and blockade and rises with victories and defense of home soil.
- Diplomacy: relations, alliances, guarantees, non-aggression pacts, war declarations with a casus belli, and peace deals priced in war score.
- Neutrality: nations may stay out. Violating a guaranteed nation's borders triggers the guarantor's join decision.

### Economic
- Province output: manpower, industry, food, gold from population and development.
- Nation treasury, monthly budget (tax rate, army upkeep, spending on building and recruiting).
- Mobilization level scales manpower pool against economic output, so mobilizing is costly.
- Blockade: naval control of sea zones cuts trade income and food.

### Military
- Land units: infantry, cavalry, artillery, grouped into armies with a general.
- Combat uses strength, terrain, entrenchment, and morale. Entrenchment makes defense strong, which produces WW1-style stalemates.
- Supply: armies need supply from owned or occupied provinces, and range limits offensives.
- Sieges and occupation: provinces have a controller separate from the owner.
- Navy (basic): fleets control sea zones, escort or block transport.

### AI
- Utility-scored goals each tick: defend, attack, mobilize, ally, make peace.
- Per-nation traits (aggression, caution). A war plan layer, for example France's offensive plan and Germany's Schlieffen-style opening, expressed as data weights rather than scripts.

## Map and data pipeline
This is the biggest piece of work.

**Files (all under `res://data/map/`)**
- `provinces.png`: each province is a unique flat RGB color. The source of truth, and hand-editable in any image editor.
- `provinces.tsv`: `id, r, g, b, name` (tab-separated).
- `terrain.png`: terrain type per pixel (or per province in `province_terrain.csv`).
- `rivers.png`, `heightmap.png`: optional visual and combat modifiers.
- `adjacencies.tsv`: auto-generated from `provinces.png` by an editor tool, plus manual overrides (straits, sea crossings).
- `sea_zones`: provinces flagged as sea.

**Rendering:** a shader samples `provinces.png` and a lookup texture (province id to owner color) to paint political color, borders and selection. Updating ownership means writing a few pixels in the lookup texture, not redrawing the map.

**Scale:** 878 provinces from the Atlantic to the Urals, 7857x5152 bitmap (see Roadmap phase 5, step 0).

**Real data sources:** Natural Earth (boundaries, rivers, terrain), historical 1914 border references, census and population figures for 1910-1914, order-of-battle references for 1914 armies.

## Architecture (Godot 4.7)
- Simulation separate from presentation: `GameState` holds all data, and the map and UI only read it.
- `Clock` autoload emitting `day_tick`, `month_tick`, `year_tick`.
- Command pattern for all player and AI actions (save/load and testing for free).
- Content as data: nations, scenarios, units and costs in JSON/`.tres`, with no hard-coded values.
- Planned layout:
  - `scripts/sim/` simulation (state, economy, military, diplomacy, AI)
  - `scripts/map/` rendering, selection, map modes, camera
  - `scripts/ui/` HUD, panels
  - `data/` map bitmaps, nations, scenarios
  - `tools/` one-off scripts (adjacency builder, map validator)

## Roadmap
Status: phases 1 to 5 are built. Next is phase 6, the AI.

1. **Map foundation.** *(done)* Province bitmap, shader rendering, selection, camera, adjacency builder, map modes (political, terrain).
2. **Sim core.** *(done)* Clock, `GameState`, nation and province data, the 1914 scenario loader, and the top bar HUD.
3. **Economy.** *(done)* `Economy` (scripts/sim/economy.gd) derives per-province GDP, industry, food and recruits from population density, the owner's development, province buildings and national programs, then each nation's monthly budget: tax revenue (with a drag above the default rate), civil, army, navy, food-import and program costs. Growth comes from a baseline rate plus the Education program. **Buildings** (farmland, factory, railway, port, trading house; up to 3 levels) are paid up front, take days to build, and are built from the Construction tab for the selected province; construction is lost if the province changes hands. Ports need a coastal province (`data/map/coastal.tsv`, from `tools/build_coastal.py`). Ports and trading houses create trade income, which a blockade removes. **Resources** (coal, iron ore, timber; thousand tonnes per year) have a per-province deposit (`data/scenarios/july_1914/resources.tsv`, built by `tools/build_resources.py` from 1913 geography and national output). Coal mines, iron mines and sawmills (level 2 at the start) set how much of a deposit is extracted. Each nation balances production against demand from industry, households and its own buildings (factories and railways burn coal and iron): shortfalls are imported and surpluses exported, with the state taking half the trade value, and a stockpile buffers a blockade. When a blockade has drained the stockpile, shortages cut industry and output. Buildings need materials, drawn from the stockpile with any shortfall bought at a markup (and impossible under blockade), so resource-poor nations build dearer. The Resources tab shows each balance. The Construction tab can **mass build** one level of any building in many provinces at once (`MassConstructBuilding`): best provinces first (biggest deposit for mines, most output otherwise), up to a province limit and a spending limit, stopping when money runs out, with an exact preview. The Buildings tab shows what each type earns and produces, and a queue with progress bars is always on screen at bottom-left (click a row to jump to the province). **Programs** (agricultural subsidies, industrial subsidies, education) are switched on and off for a running cost. **Mobilization** lives in the Military panel (M): it trades output for manpower. Food shortage and `Nation.blockade` (set by the navy in phase 7) cut output. All changes go through commands (`SetEconomyPolicy`, `ConstructBuilding`, `SetProgram`) via `GameState.issue()`. Constants and building/program definitions are in `data/economy.json`; per-nation `development` and `agri_yield` are calibrated in the scenario. Economy panel: E. Tests: `tests/economy_test.gd`.
   *Map modes:* buttons at the bottom right (and keys 1-6) switch between Political, Resources (coal, iron or timber deposits), Income (tax per province), Population, Buildings (levels of all buildings or of one type) and Provinces (debug colours). Modes with a choice show a submode picker, and shaded modes show a legend; pressing a mode's key again steps through its submodes. Dynamic modes (Income, Buildings) repaint when the economy changes. The camera ignores the scroll wheel while the cursor is over any UI control, because Godot lets wheel events through even stop-filtered panels; `tests/ui_input_test.gd` checks this in a real window.
   *Economy UI conventions:* every figure is per month (money in GBP millions, resource flows in kt/Mt; stockpiles are plain amounts) and comes from `Fmt` (scripts/ui/fmt.gd). The Economy panel (E) has three tabs: **Overview** (problems list, tax rate, grouped income and spending, national programs), **Resources** (food, coal, iron, timber: made, used, balance, stock), **Buildings** (one table of every building type with a Build button for the selected province; mass build folded away). Detail lives in tooltips. Food imports cost the treasury only a tenth of their value (`food_state_share`) because the whole economy buys food.
4. **Military.** *(done)* Divisions grouped into armies that march province to province, fight, dig in, draw supply and occupy. Rules are in `Military` (scripts/sim/military.gd), constants in `data/military.json`, units in `Unit`/`Army`. Tests: `tests/military_test.gd` (sim) and `tests/military_ui_test.gd` (UI, headless).
   *Units:* infantry division (12,000 men), cavalry division (4,000), artillery brigade (3,000). A unit has strength (share of its full size) and morale. Armies hold units and a general (a power multiplier). 90% of each nation's `peacetime_army` starts in the field, placed by `deployment` in the scenario file (garrisons and frontier districts, dealt out by weight); the other 10% is the recruit reserve (`Nation.manpower`). `Nation.total_men()` is reserve plus men under arms and in training, and is what upkeep and the mobilization cap apply to. **Recruiting** (`RaiseUnits`) pays recruits and money up front, takes training days, then the unit joins an army in that province.
   *Terrain:* `data/map/terrain.tsv` (from `tools/build_terrain.py`, hand-curated per province since no elevation data is bundled): plains, forest, hills, mountains, marsh, urban. It sets defence, movement cost and siege time.
   *Movement:* province to province along land borders and strait links (open-sea links wait for the navy). Days per step come from centre distance, the slowest unit's speed, destination terrain, and railways in provinces you hold. Armies may only enter their own ground, ground they hold, or ground of a nation they are at war with, so neutral borders are closed until diplomacy adds passage rights. Orders (`MoveArmy`) take the cheapest path.
   *Combat:* every day, hostile armies in one province fight. Each side's power (men in thousands times type attack, dulled by low morale, times the general) becomes enemy casualties, about 1% of the men a day for equal armies. The army that was there first defends: terrain defence times entrenchment (+12% a level, up to 5 levels, one level per 4 days standing still; moving resets it) divides its casualties. Casualties wear morale down; below 25% the army routs to the best neighbouring province (10% extra loss) or is lost if it has none.
   *Supply:* an army is supplied within 3 hops of ground its nation holds (a railway hop counts half). Supplied armies recover morale and refill from the reserve; unsupplied ones lose strength and morale daily.
   *Occupation:* an army alone in a province held by a nation it is at war with takes control after a siege (terrain's `occupy_days`; the province's owner does not change). The occupier collects a quarter of the province's GDP as tax; the owner loses its output and recruits, but its people still need feeding. An army of the owner retakes it the same way.
   *Routs and collapse:* a beaten army only falls back into a neighbouring province its nation still holds; with none it is cut off and lost, so a defeated army cannot slip onto its own occupied land and retake it for free. A nation that holds no province at all loses every army and all training at once (`GameState.nation_collapsed`).
   *War state and records:* `GameState.wars` (active `War` objects, keyed by nation pair) with `at_war()`, `war_between()`, `wars_of()` and `war_history`; `SetWar` (the Military panel's War section) starts and ends wars for testing until phase 5 replaces it. A `War` (scripts/sim/war.gd) records who started it and when, each side's losses in battle, to attrition and cut off or disbanded, provinces and people taken, every finished battle (place, dates, attacker, winner, starting strengths, losses) and a dated log of battles, captures, surrenders, collapses, declarations and peace. Battles are tracked per province (`GameState.engagements`) and written into the war when no hostile armies share the province any more; the winner is the one side left standing. The Military panel's **Wars** tab lists active wars (yours first) and past ones, with a table of both sides' results and a filterable log; double-click an entry to jump to where it happened.
   *UI:* army banners on the map (men in thousands, red outline while fighting, yellow when selected, selected armies' routes drawn). Clicking a province selects your armies there and, if it holds several armies, fans them out as individual named banners (all nations' armies, yours first): click one banner to select just that army, Shift-click to add or remove one. Under the name of every fanned-out army, and under any selected army, small unit symbols with counts show its composition (`Army.composition`). Right-click another province to march. The Military panel (M) lists armies with merge, split in half and split off by unit type (pick infantry, cavalry or artillery and how many; at least one unit stays behind), recruiting, and the war switch. Map modes 7 (Terrain) and 8 (Military: who holds each province, occupied ones darkened). An event feed at the top centre reports battles, captures, wars and new units.
   *Selection and symbols:* dragging a box on the map selects every one of your armies inside it (Shift adds to the selection). Banners show a NATO-style symbol (infantry, cavalry, artillery, or one the player sets: guard, fortress garrison, reserve; `SetArmyIcon`), the men in thousands, and a size mark (X, XX, XXX) from `size_marks` in `data/military.json`.
   *Route times on the map:* the selected armies' routes show the days to reach each province and the arrival date; other marching armies show their destination; hovering a province with armies selected previews the route and its time before you right-click.
   *Fronts:* a front (`Front`, `DesignateFront`, `AssignToFront`, `DeleteFront`) is the set of provinces to hold. The usual way to make one is "Front vs nation" in the Military panel: click a province of the neighbouring nation and the front is every province you hold that touches ground that nation holds (`Military.border_provinces`). It follows the war: the line is re-read daily, so conquests and losses move it. "Draw line" makes a hand-drawn chain of bordering provinces instead. Armies assigned to a front are dealt out along it by strength (`Military.step_fronts`), walk to their province and dig in; a manual order releases an army from its front, and destroyed armies drop off it. Fronts are drawn on the map with their names and army counts.
   *Selection:* click a province to select your armies there; click it again, click ground without your armies, press Esc, or use the Deselect button to clear the selection.
   *Occupation on the map:* the Political mode stripes an occupied province with the occupier's colour over the owner's (shader `occupation_stripes`); the Military mode paints it solid in the occupier's colour.
   *Battle screen:* every battle keeps a full account (`Military._engagement`, read with `GameState.battle_by_id` / `battle_at`): terrain, dates, attacker and winner; per side the armies, generals, starting and current or final composition, men, morale and the defender's terrain-and-trench multiplier; a day-by-day timeline of men, losses and morale; and events (who joined, who routed or was cut off, who held the field). Battles in progress are in `GameState.engagements`; finished ones go to `battle_archive` (the newest 300), and the war record points at them by id. While a battle is on, a red crossed-swords icon with the day count sits beside the province's banners on the map; clicking it, or double-clicking a battle in the Wars tab's Battles list (or in its log), opens the battle screen (`BattleScreen`, with `BattleChart` for the strength and morale lines). An open screen updates daily for a battle in progress and shows the result once it ends.
   *Not yet:* alliances and passage rights (phase 5), AI-driven armies (phase 6), sea transport and naval supply (phase 7), war score from occupation (phase 5).
5. **Diplomacy and war.** *(done, steps 0 to 8 below)* Relations, alliances, guarantees, war declarations, peace deals.

   **Starting point (what phase 4 left us).** `GameState.wars` is pairwise (`A|B` keys), `SetWar` is a testing stand-in, and `Military.can_enter` only allows own ground, held ground or an enemy's. `scenario.json` already carries `blocs`, `neutral_pacts` and `guarantees` data that nothing reads yet. There is no Russia, and no war score, war support or casus belli. `Nation.blockade` is still a placeholder for phase 7.

   **Design decisions.**
   - Wars stay pairwise (`War` is one record per enemy pair). An alliance war is several pairwise wars opened together, so combat, occupation and the war record need no rewrite. A shared `war_group` id on each `War` ties the pairs of one conflict, and the peace screen groups them by it.
   - A nation can make a separate peace; allies are not dragged along. Allies who stop fighting lose trust with the rest of the bloc (see Relations).
   - Everything goes through commands. Diplomacy state lives in a new `Diplomacy` rules class (static, like `Economy` and `Military`) acting on `GameState`; `DiplomacyState` data sits in `GameState` so save/load needs nothing new.
   - Constants and definitions in `data/diplomacy.json`; the starting web of treaties comes from the scenario file.

   **Data model.**
   - `GameState.relations`: sorted pair key to opinion, -100..100 (starts from the scenario, drifts toward a baseline).
   - `GameState.treaties`: array of `Treaty` `{id, kind, a, b, start_day, end_day}` with kinds `alliance`, `guarantee` (one way: a guarantees b), `nap` (non-aggression), `passage` (one way: a lets b's armies cross), `neutral_pact` (Italy's Triple Alliance opt-out clause).
   - `Nation` gains `war_support` (0-100), `prestige`, `stability` is left out of v1. `War` gains `war_group`, `casus_belli`, `score` (per side, see below) and `occupied_share`.
   - `GameState.mobilization_calls`: tags that have been called up by an alliance this crisis, used for the join rules.

   **Systems, in build order.** Each step ends with its own tests and is playable on its own.
   0. **Map expansion.** *(done)* The map now runs from the Atlantic to the Urals (lon -11 to 61, lat 34.8 to 67) and covers the whole Balkans: 7857x5152 px, 878 provinces (was 3328x3712, 556). Existing province ids, colours and names did not change; new provinces were appended (ids 557-717 Balkans, 718-878 Russia). Built by `tools/extend_map.py <natural-earth admin-1> <slice>`, which pastes the old bitmap into the larger canvas and paints one slice at a time (`balkans`, then `russia`); it also extends provinces the old frame had cut (East Prussia, Slovakia, Hungary, Scotland, Crete's strip) and writes an `admin` column (modern country) into `provinces.tsv` for the new rows. Slice definitions (owners by country and unit, 1914 border fixes, renames, merge/split limits) are the `SLICES` dict in that script. After a slice: `build_adjacencies.py`, `build_coastal.py`, `build_terrain.py`, `build_population.py <populated places>`, `build_resources.py`, then recalibrate (`tools/calibrate_nations.gd`, see below). The population and resource builders keep a separate national total per slice (`GROUPS`, ids by range), so adding a region never changes the numbers of an older one.
      *New nations (tags in `data/nations.json`):* SRB, MNE, ALB, GRE, BUL, ROM, OTT (European Thrace and Constantinople only) and RUS (European Russia, the Urals, the North Caucasus, Finland, the Baltic provinces, Poland east of the Prussian partition, Belarus, Ukraine without Galicia, Moldova). Austria-Hungary now includes Galicia, Bukovina, Carpathian Ruthenia, Transylvania, the Banat and Bačka, and all of Hungary. Kaliningrad and Memel belong to Germany. Transcaucasia, Anatolia, Kazakhstan, Norway and Sweden are grey background land (not playable). The map is cut at lat 67 and lon 61, so Lapland, Murmansk and the Arctic are truncated.
      *Scenario data for the new nations:* development, agri_yield, intensity, army, treasury, deployments and generals are in `scenario.json` and `military.json`. Each nation's economy is calibrated by `tools/calibrate_nations.gd` (prints GDP per head, revenue, food balance, resource demand and net budget for every nation; run `godot --headless --path . -s res://tools/calibrate_nations.gd`). Targets: GDP per head about 12-22 GBP for the new nations (relative to Italy 28), revenue 150 for Austria-Hungary and 280 for Germany (as `economy_test.gd` checks), peacetime armies sized so no nation starts in deficit (a small nation cannot afford its historical army at 70 GBP a man a year, so mobilization has to supply the rest).
      *Known simplifications:* the scenario `area` column is equal-area pixels (scaled by cos(latitude) against the 47 deg of the original frame), so densities stay comparable; the old west still has two oddities carried over from the first frame (Polish units outside the Prussian partition were once labelled German; only Lodz 1 and 2 were corrected to Russian). Province borders follow modern administrative units; Russian provinces are modern oblasts named after the governorate they roughly match. Iron ore, coal and timber for the new regions are rough national totals spread by weight.

Queries: `are_allied`, `guarantors_of`, `has_passage`, `find_treaty`, `add_treaty`, `end_treaty`. Not wired into `Military.can_enter` yet (step 2). Russian mobilization is left to step 8. Tests: `tests/diplomacy_test.gd`. Treaty targets to seed: Serbia-Russia, Austria's war on Serbia already under way at the start, Russian mobilization, Romania/Bulgaria/Greece/Ottoman neutral at start with hooks for later joins. Remove ITA from the Central Powers bloc: it starts neutral.
   2. **Declarations and casus belli.** *(done)* `DeclareWar` (a, b, casus belli, optional war group) goes through `Diplomacy.declare_war`; `casus_belli_options(a, b)` lists what a nation can use today: `reconquest` and `border_claim` from the scenario's `claims` (province names; a border claim needs the province to touch the claimant), `guarantee_defence` (the target is at war with a nation you guarantee), `ally_called` (an ally is at war with the target), `unprovoked_aggression` (earned, dated) and always `none`. `declare_block` gives the reason a declaration is impossible: same, unknown, no_land, at_war, allied (an entente counts) or nap. A NAP is torn up first with `CancelTreaty` (costs the partner's opinion, everyone's opinion and prestige; costs per treaty kind in `data/diplomacy.json`, `war.cancel`). Each casus belli sets the target's opinion drop, the world's and prestige (`war.casus_belli`); `none` costs more and gives the aggressor's land neighbours and the victim's guarantors an `unprovoked_aggression` casus belli for five years plus 40 grievance (`GameState.casus_belli`, `GameState.grievance`; grievance cools 2 a month). A provoked AI nation (grievance 60+, with the casus belli) declares on the aggressor in the monthly step; the player never does it by rule. `War` records `casus_belli` and `war_group`; `Nation.prestige` is new. `Military.can_enter` now lets allies and passage-treaty holders cross (owner and holder must both allow it; ententes do not open land). Not done: join decisions for allies (step 3), the mobilization gate on unjustified wars (step 4), border-crossing and mobilization provocation (steps 3-4), a UI (step 7); the Military panel still uses `SetWar`. Tests: `tests/diplomacy_test.gd`. Original plan: `DeclareWar` command replaces `SetWar` as the way wars start (`SetWar` stays as a test/debug command). Needs a casus belli (`border_claim`, `guarantee_defence`, `ally_called`, `reconquest`, or `none` with a relations and war support penalty). Validity rules: not allied, no active NAP (breaking one costs relations with everyone and prestige), not already at war, and neutral borders stay closed until the war exists. Declaration runs the join logic below. Add `Military.can_enter` support for allies (passage through allied ground) and `passage` treaties.
   3. **Alliances and joining.** *(done)* Every declaration opens a conflict record (`GameState.war_groups`: original attacker and defender, members by side). `Diplomacy.offer_joins` asks every ally and guarantor of the members; each nation that joins opens pairwise wars (cb `ally_called` or `guarantee_defence`, same `war_group`) against every member of the other side and then brings its own allies in. Obligations: a guarantee binds when the guaranteed nation is the original target; an alliance binds as `alliance_defence` when the ally is the original target (defensive-only ones too) and as `alliance_called` otherwise unless it is defensive-only; an entente is optional (the AI joins if its opinion of an enemy is at or below `join.entente_join_relation`). Declining a bound obligation costs the ally's goodwill and prestige and, for defence and guarantees, ends the treaty (`join.decline` in `data/diplomacy.json`). A nation that would have to fight its own ally or pact partner declines for free. The AI always answers at once; the player gets a pending decision (`GameState.pending_joins`, signal `join_requested`) carrying the enemies and the price of each answer, `Clock` refuses to run while one waits, and `AnswerJoin` settles it. `Diplomacy.violate_border(violator, victim)` asks a guaranteed nation's guarantors without a war between the two; nothing calls it yet because armies cannot enter neutral ground. The starting Austro-Serbian war is registered as a conflict but calls no one: `offer_joins` is for step 4's crisis loop. Anglo-Portuguese alliance is defensive-only. Tests: `tests/diplomacy_test.gd`. Original plan: On a declaration, every ally of the target is offered a join decision (the player gets a prompt; the AI decides by rules): defensive alliances join automatically if the target was attacked, offensive ones only if called by the leader. Guarantors of the target get the join decision too (the Belgium case). Joining opens pairwise wars against the aggressor's side with `casus_belli = ally_called`/`guarantee_defence` and the same `war_group`. Declining breaks the obligation (relations penalty with the ally; a broken guarantee ends the treaty and costs prestige). Violation of a guaranteed nation's borders without a war declaration (an army entering Belgian ground) triggers the same decision.
   4. **Mobilization and the crisis loop.** *(done)* `Diplomacy.step_day` runs daily (constants in `data/diplomacy.json`, `crisis`). A nation at 20% mobilization alarms each land neighbour it is neither allied with nor at war with, once (`GameState.alarms`): their opinion of it drops 10, they resent it (+15 grievance) and the neighbour's allies are called up (`GameState.mobilization_calls`, entente partners excepted). AI nations answer: 60% of whoever alarms them, 80% of whoever alarmed an ally, 50% when called to a war, and a floor of 50% at war; they ease back 1% a day when nothing threatens them, and move up 5% a day. The player's own slider is never touched except by the war floor. Mobilization changes recompute the economy. AI nations bound to join a war no longer enter at once: they are *called* (`GameState.join_calls`, signal `join_called`), mobilize, and enter when they reach 40% (about eight days), then call their own allies. Player decisions are still immediate prompts. A war with no casus belli needs 30% mobilization first (`Diplomacy.declare_gate`), so aggression shows as an alarm before it happens. With the scenario's `start_crisis` the starting Austro-Serbian war calls Russia, Montenegro and Germany on day one and the general war follows from the rules alone (France is prompted when Russia enters). The default player's economy therefore starts to feel mobilization within days; set `start_crisis` to false in `scenario.json` for a quiet start. Tests: `tests/diplomacy_test.gd`. Original plan: Mobilization (already in Economy) becomes a visible diplomatic act: raising it above a threshold next to a neighbour lowers their relations and lets their allies call up too. Countries at war get war-time rules (mobilization floor, war support effects). A declaration can only happen after `mobilization >= cfg.min_mobilization` for aggressors with no casus belli, so a war cannot start from nothing and the 1914 chain can start, or not start, from systems alone.
   5. **War score and peace.** *(done, except war support: step 6)* Rules live in `Peace` (scripts/sim/peace.gd), constants in `data/diplomacy.json` (`peace`), commands `ProposePeace` and `AnswerPeace`. **Score** is one number per pairwise war, -100..100, the same from both sides with the sign flipped: ground held (100 times the share of the other nation's population and GDP, half each), +/-25 for holding a capital (`Nation.capital`, set per nation in `scenario.json`), and battles won (0.15 a thousand men the loser lost, scaled by how lopsided, capped at 10, fading 2% a month; `Military` credits them as it files each battle). **Offers:** `Peace.propose(from, to, mode, demands)`; in `demand` mode the proposer takes the demands from the target, in `offer` mode it gives them; no demands is a white peace. Demands: `province` (priced at 100 times its share of the giver's weight, half if the taker holds it, double for a capital), `indemnity` (share of revenue and months, paid monthly from a fixed instalment, `GameState.indemnities`), `release_ally` (ends an alliance or guarantee), `disarm_border` (the giver's border provinces closed to its armies for N years while at peace, `GameState.restrictions`, enforced in `Military.can_enter`). The taker can only ask for what its score pays for, unless the giver has lost its capital to it. **Answers:** an AI giver refuses while its own score is above -5; a white peace is refused while the answerer's score is above 20; an AI offered terms it can afford takes them. The player gets a pending offer (`GameState.pending_offers`, signal `peace_offered`, `Clock` refuses to run) and answers with `AnswerPeace`. A refusal starts a 30-day cooldown for the proposer. Each month a losing AI (score -30) offers the ground its enemy holds, up to 80% of the enemy's score, and a stalemate of nine months (score within 15) offers white peace. **Making peace** (`Peace.make_peace`) carries out the terms through `GameState.transfer_province`, hands ground that was only held back to its owner, withdraws armies from ground they may no longer stand on to the nearest ground they may, redraws fronts, files the terms in the war record (`War.peace`), and treats a peace made while allies still fight the same enemy as a desertion (-25 relations with each, -5 prestige); a nation with no enemies left leaves the conflict. A ceded capital moves to the most populous remaining province. Not done: war support (a nation whose support is gone being forced to a white peace) is step 6; `Diplomacy.peace_value` of the AI hooks is `Peace.war_score`, `Diplomacy.acceptable` is `Peace.acceptable`. Tests: `tests/peace_test.gd`. Original plan: War score accrues per war: +occupation of provinces by population/GDP share, +battles won (scaled by losses), +holding the enemy capital, -own provinces lost, and decays slowly. `ProposePeace` (white peace, or a deal priced in score) and `AcceptPeace`/`RefusePeace` commands. Demands and their price: take province (price from population and GDP), demand indemnity (a share of treasury and monthly income, paid over years), demand a treaty clause (disarm border, ally release), release a vassal is left out. A nation refuses while it still wins and war support is above its floor; war support below a threshold forces a white peace, and a nation that holds no capital or whose war support hits 0 can be forced into a surrender at a price of everything demanded. Province transfers go through `GameState.transfer_province`, occupation of the transferred area is dropped, and unit fronts are redrawn.
   6. **War support.** *(done)* `WarSupport` (scripts/sim/war_support.gd), constants in `data/diplomacy.json` (`war_support`). `Nation.war_support` (0-100) starts from the scenario's figure or the government's default (constitutional monarchy 60, republic 55) and returns there in peace at 3 a month. At war it falls monthly by 0.5 weariness, 150 times the share of its men lost that month (battle losses, attrition and surrenders, `Nation.men_lost_month`), 10 times the blockade, and 20 times the share of its people under enemy control; a battle won lifts it 2 (3 on its own soil) and a battle lost lowers it 1.5. A war with no casus belli costs 10, a just cause (reconquest, guarantee, unprovoked-aggression claim) gives 5. **Effects, while at war only:** `economy_mult` scales tax (`Economy.nation_budget`) and monthly recruitment (0.7 at zero support, 1.0 from 50 up) and `morale_mult` scales the fighting power of the nation's armies in `Military._fight` (0.75 at zero, 1.0 at 50, 1.1 at 100). **Weary nations:** at or below 25 an AI nation takes any terms it can be made to pay and sues for peace (white peace, or the ground held if it is losing); at or below 15 it is forced into a white peace with every enemy it is not clearly losing to (score of -20 or better, even if it is winning), while a nation that is also clearly losing stays at war so the enemy can dictate terms; at zero it can be made to give anything, as with a lost capital. Support is recalculated and the economy refreshed monthly, in `Diplomacy.step_month`. Signal `war_support_changed`. Tests: `tests/war_support_test.gd`. Original plan: Per nation 0-100: starts from government and scenario, goes down with casualties (men lost per month as a share of total), blockade and occupied provinces; goes up with victories and defending home soil. It scales recruitment, tax and morale in `Military` and `Economy` through two multipliers read from `data/diplomacy.json`, and hits 0 forces peace as in step 5.
